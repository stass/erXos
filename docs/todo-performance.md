# Performance watch list

Places where erXos may be slow, written down so they are measured before
they are forgotten. Each item names the cost, where it sits, the
measurement that settles it (a `tools/erx bench` workload, existing or to
be written) and the fixes on the table. An item is a suspicion, not a
finding: no item changes a design until a measurement says so.

When a measurement is taken, record its result with what
[CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) requires (host, accelerator, QEMU version,
compiler, flags, workload, baseline) and remove the item: either the cost
is acceptable, or the fix becomes a design change and a `TODO.md` or
roadmap item. Latency and fairness that a stopgap does not promise are
residuals of the component that replaces it, not items here, unless they
block a milestone's numbers. A number marked "probe" is a one-off reading,
not a recorded measurement. Items quote numbers in short; each number's
full conditions (means or medians, baseline commit and the history commit
of its run) are in the full list as it stood before it was grouped,
`~/erxos-history/docs-archive/todo-performance.md` at history commit
`e38149b`.

## Kernel and scheduler
* **[Medium] kernel, first-use hash of large boot image files.** The kernel checks each boot image file's BLAKE3 hash at its first map or read with the portable code, since it executes no FP or SIMD instruction (K10 in [design-kernel.md](design-kernel.md)); a large file mapped on demand, such as ML weights, pays the full portable cost at its first map. Measure portable against NEON throughput on QEMU `virt` (HVF) and the Pi 4, and a large file's first-map latency, once boot image format version 2 lands. Fixes: one bracketed kernel routine that saves the thread's FP state and hashes with NEON, the only exception to K10's scan; or checking the largest files in `bootstrap`, which may use NEON.
* **[Low] kernel, BLAKE3's round function out of line.** `kernel/crypto/blake3/blake3_impl.h` defines `INLINE` as `static inline`, not upstream's `always_inline`, which made a 5280-byte frame at -O0 that the stack proof refuses; at -O2 clang then calls `round_fn` seven times per compression with its state in memory. Measure portable throughput at -O2, both forms, over the boot image's file sizes on QEMU `virt` (HVF), with the -O0 and -O2 frames the stack proof reports. Fixes: `always_inline` at -O2 alone, or the seven rounds unrolled by hand in `erx_blake3_compress_in_place`.
* **[Medium] kernel, a walk of the group path at every decision.** The scheduler core (`kernel/sched/sched.c`) stores no derived limit, so each wake, pick and examined thread loads the ceiling and the cpuset of every level of its group path (up to eight each; twice for a pick, for urgency and for CPUs) from records other CPUs read. Measure `tools/erx bench` park and wake on one and across CPUs at group depths 1, 4 and 8 on QEMU `virt` (HVF). Fixes: one walk per thread per decision yielding both limits, or a per-group generation that lets a thread keep its limits until an ancestor's grant changes.
* **[Low] kernel, a ready queue's size per CPU.** A CPU's ready queues are 64 pairs of pointers beside the bitmap, a little over 1 KiB, and a pick reads two cache lines of them. Measure context-switch latency with ready threads at one level and spread over all 64, on QEMU `virt` (HVF), with G1's numbers. Fixes: keep only the head and thread the tail through it, or compact the levels.
* **[Low] kernel, eager FP and SIMD switch.** Every switch saves and restores the full FP and SIMD state; the `TODO.md` item on the eager switch's missing cases tracks the `tools/erx bench` workload that times it.
* **[Medium] scheduler, the cost of one machine-wide scheduling lock.** The fallback to the cross-CPU protocol of [design-scheduler.md](design-scheduler.md#concurrency) serializes every switch and wake through one lock held across the stack switch: an estimated 100 to 200 ns hold per switch against an estimated 150 to 250 ns IPC round trip ([design-kernel.md](design-kernel.md)), so on four CPUs it may dominate IPC-heavy work. Measure, only if the protocol fails its model or implementation: a contended ticket lock across a switch on four vCPUs under HVF with the IPC workloads, against M7's gate. While the protocol stands, no fix is needed.
* **[Low] kernel, a part of a range call goes through a tree split and join.** `erx_map_apply` (`kernel/core/map.c`) takes every edit out of the tree by two splits and back by joins, about ten walks of a path of up to 23 records of about 100 bytes under the space's lock, where a boundary move inside one record needs one walk up; a 1 GiB commit does this 4,096 times. Measure `tools/erx bench mmap` commits and protects over a space with many records. Fix: an in-place path, behind the same plan, for a plan that keeps its records.
* **[Low] kernel, admission as a step machine.** Every kernel call fills its admission plan (`erx_sys_admit_plan`, sized for the ABI's largest call) and makes one out-of-line `erx_sys_admit_next` per lookup, lock, run and release (`kernel/core/admit.c`): three calls and a plan copy on the null call. Measure the `null` workload once the dispatch loop lands, against inlined admission. Fixes: the dispatch loop and the core in one translation unit at -O2, or straight-line per-call steps emitted from the plan.
* **[Low] kernel, one lock per retirement in the grace tracker.** Every object put and record free retires alone through `erx_grace_retire` (`kernel/core/grace.c`), which takes the tracker's one lock to read and advance the period. Under many CPUs freeing at once that lock may contend. Measure with `tools/erx bench stop` at 4 CPUs; if it shows, stamp a CPU's whole batch of freed records once, as FreeBSD's UMA does with its SMR buckets (`sys/vm/uma_core.c`, `sys/kern/subr_smr.c`).
* **[Low] kernel, the page allocator under many CPUs.** The design does not say whether the buddy allocator (`erx_kalloc` and the page records, [design-kernel.md](design-kernel.md)) takes one lock or keeps per-CPU page caches, as FreeBSD's does through UMA's cache zones (`sys/vm/vm_page.c`). Measure page allocation and free at 4 CPUs under `tools/erx bench stop`; if the lock shows, per-CPU caches of free pages, each still charged to the budget that takes a page.
* **[Low] kernel, a destroy's slot names.** `erx_cap_slot_name` (`kernel/core/cap.c`) finds a slot's index by scanning the table's directory, up to `ERX_CAPS_MAX / ERX_CAP_PAGE_SLOTS` page pointers, for every slot a teardown clears. Measure a destroy of an object named by many slots in a full table. Fixes: store each page's first index in its slots, or align pages so the index follows from the address.
* **Principals whose CPU caps oversubscribe `users` contend by round robin** (`docs/design-scheduler.md`, the L1 pick function). The manifest holds each principal's grant within `users` without summing them, so two principals each capped at all of `users` split it by round robin over their threads, and the one with more threads takes more. Settled by the measurement of the open question "Is round robin within a level enough?" (E-S1, E-S9): two principals granted `users`, one runnable thread against many, CPU time each receives. Fix: a fair queue behind the pick function.

## Fiasco backend and the root service

* **[High] 0.2 on Fiasco, the cost of hardening Fiasco's entry against branch-predictor injection (FD32).** The patch of [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#the-releases-fiasco-build) calls the firmware's `SMCCC_ARCH_WORKAROUND_1` and `_3` at every entry from user mode and every address-space switch, so every system call and root round trip pays a call into BL31 on the Pi 4's Cortex-A72; its size is unverified. The fork carries the patch only if it costs nothing measurable; otherwise FD32 stays a published downside. Settled by `tools/erx bench`'s null request ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#measurements)) natively on a Pi 4, two release builds with and without the patch, several runs each: no cost when the patched median lies within the spread of the unpatched medians. QEMU numbers do not settle it.
* **[Low] bench, `nullcall`'s `kernel` row compares a long mean with a short window.** The erXos cell's mean covers 640,000 calls over about 30 ms and takes in every host interruption, so it moves from run to run (60.1 ns in a run with one 1.7 ms batch, 46.5 ns in the next, both on `6f199667b`), while `syscallbench` prints one window of 10,000 calls, about 0.34 ms, with no warm-up. The side-by-side table puts the two side by side unqualified. Settle by reading, for the comparison, the erXos cell's median of 10,000-call windows beside the L4Re row, and decide in [design-bench.md](design-bench.md) whether the table shows it.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, the eager FP switch in a same-space round trip.** Probe on upstream Fiasco `07ea18e1` (M3 Max, HVF, 1 CPU, gcc 12.2.0), built with and without `LAZY_FPU`: `ipcbench`'s same-space round trip costs 353.2 and 364.3 cycles eager against 313.0 and 319.8 lazy, about 14 ns more, while the cross-space round trip shows no increase (548.2 and 553.0 eager, 557.0 and 560.4 lazy). The fork switches eagerly by design ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md), "Eager FP switching"), so this is the price of that choice in erXos's same-space cells; why the cross-space trip does not show it is unexplained. Settle with more than two runs of each build before reading the difference.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, a doorbell from ring to wake** (`liberx/backend/fiasco`, [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#measurements)): the holder triggers its own IRQ capability and makes no request (`erx_irq_trigger`, `service_stubs.c`), so what is left is Fiasco's delivery to the root's dispatcher and the wake of the parked executor, on one CPU or across CPUs. Settled by `tools/erx bench wake`'s `trigger-take` under HVF ([design-bench.md](design-bench.md#1-wake-how-late-a-waiting-thread-runs)).
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, what the stop adds to paths that always run** ([design-debugger.md](design-debugger.md#performance)): a store of the interrupted frame's address per CPU at every interrupt entry, so a CPU that takes the stop request can publish the registers it interrupted, and a load of the stop word on each pass of a contended spinlock's wait. Settled by `tools/erx bench` before and after the stop lands: `nullcall`'s and `wake`'s rows for the interrupt entry, and a contended-lock row for the wait, under HVF and on the Pi 4. If the entry's store shows, the stop reads the frame from the handler's own argument instead and keeps nothing per CPU.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, a held board's memory at the console's rate** ([design-debugger.md](design-debugger.md#the-hold)): the hold answers on the console line, at 115,200 baud about 8 KiB of memory a second through base64 (an estimate), so `report` on a held board reads for about a minute and `whatis` on a physical address, which reads every space's tables, for much longer. Settled by timing `tools/erx debug --tty DEVICE report` and `audit` on the Pi 4 with a seeded stop. Fixes on the table: a faster rate for the hold alone, set by the stop on a UART it already owns; replies that leave out pages of zeros; a second line.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, the urgent scheduler's kernel entries** (ERTS's urgent scheduler, [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#urgency)): another scheduler that queues it a process wakes it with a trigger of its wake bell and a thread switch, and it takes the deliveries queued at its thread with a wait whose deadline is zero, a kernel entry, at each of its process switches. Settled, once stage 3 is built, by counting both under the G5 workloads and in the Raspberry Pi 4's interrupt measurement, and timing each entry.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, a park for every idle moment** (ERTS's schedulers, [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#scheduling), "A wait blocks"): busy waiting is off in every runtime, so each wait pays the park's two kernel entries and the dispatcher's switch. Under HVF on one CPU the boot to the release shell takes a median of 818.8 ms against 642.8 ms with stock spinning (an Apple M3 Max under load, QEMU 11.1.3, clang 23.1.3, 11 boots each; history repository, `bench/2026-11-22-spin/`). The cross-domain ping-pong of `tools/erx bench xdomain` is about 3 us slower at the median, 20.0 against 16.8 us on one CPU (four boots without spinning, one of which gave 15.0, against three with it) and 20.5 against 16.2 us on four, while the posix bench's program calls are faster, a read of 4 KiB 13.5 against 24.6 us and an open 50 against 63 us on one CPU, since a spinning scheduler of the session's runtime at band 2 keeps a band-1 program off its CPU (HVF on an Apple M3 Max at a one-minute load of 3 to 7, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, stock spinning against none). Settled by the same boot on the Raspberry Pi 4, native, with `+sbwt none` against `very_short`, and by a count of parks in it; the scheduler's stage 4 is what makes a park cheaper.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, threads started with no mask share one CPU** (`liberx/backend/fiasco`, [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#scheduling)): Fiasco places a thread on its home CPU or the starter's if the mask holds it (`scheduler.cpp:96-103`) and never moves it, so ERTS schedulers started with no affinity all run on one CPU of the domain's set. Settled by a CPU-bound load across every scheduler of a domain whose set holds four CPUs, recording each CPU's busy time. Fixes: the root spreads a no-mask thread over its domain's set at start (round robin within the narrowing function), or ERTS binds its schedulers.
* **[Medium] Fiasco backend, one service thread for every call.** The root's one service thread serves every memory, thread and log call of every task and receives their faults ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#helper-threads)), so all tasks' calls queue on it; `erx_irq_trigger`, which ERTS's scheduler wakes use, is the one to watch. Measure the queue depth, per-call time and `erx_irq_trigger`'s wake latency. Fixes: serve the hot calls in the caller (a wake needs no root state) or add service threads; erXos's kernel serves these calls itself.
* **[Low] Fiasco backend, the condition variable's extra wake.** The port always takes `unlock_requeue`'s branch that wakes the next waiter, and deletes `FUTEX_REQUEUE` ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#thread-exit)), so a broadcast wakes each waiter once more than a requeue would. Measure a `pthread_cond_broadcast` over many waiters; keep the wake unless it shows in ERTS's numbers.
* **A semaphore's wake path** (`fiasco/kernel/src/kern/semaphore.cpp`, `thread_state.cpp`): an up holds one more state bit between its dequeue and its activation. Settled by timing an up that wakes a blocked down, on one CPU and across CPUs, under HVF, with the bit and without.
* **[Low] Fiasco backend, the cost of a commit in the root.** Each committed page is zeroed by `root_mem.c`'s word loop and mapped by one `l4_task_map` call. Settled by committing and touching a large range on HVF. Fixes: musl's `bzero`, and mapping runs of contiguous window pages in one call.
* **ERTS's commits natively, one page at a time** (`liberx/backend/fiasco/vm.c`). A hosted runtime has about 25,000 pages (99 MiB) committed by `1>` (`ERX_HOSTED_VM_RUNS`; macOS, Apple Silicon, Apple clang 21.0.0 -O2, interpreter build, one boot); natively each is taken, zeroed and mapped on the one service thread. Settled by timing a native boot to `1>` against its commit count, at 1 and 4 CPUs.
* [Low] root's session waits for the media domain at boot on QEMU virt: `media` is a files facet, so the session's namespace is in place only once the media domain has reported ready (`erx_domain_mints`), and a slow media domain delays the console's prompt. Settled by boot-to-prompt time on the 1-CPU QEMU virt cell under HVF with and without root's `media` facet row.
* **A task's exit over a large reservation** (`erx_backend_vm_table_clear`): it probes the page index for every reserved page, committed or not, on the one service thread. Settled by timing the stop of a task holding a 1 GiB reservation.
* **[Low] Exec, the window reserve and release walk every page of the loader's window.** `erx_exec_load` reserves the rest of the 256 MiB window (`exec_window_bytes`, `abi/protocols/prog.txt`) and the executable releases it; both pass through `vm_table.c`, about 65,500 page-index probes each on the root's service thread. Settled by an exec workload, this window against 16 MiB; shrink `exec_window_bytes` if it shows.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/core/handles.c`, `liberx/backend/fiasco/object.c`: a create scans the object records from the start for the lowest free record and a task's end scans every record, so n creates cost O(n^2); a handle entry is 24 bytes, so a page holds 170 entries and one table at most 87,040. Settled by 10,000 creates and closes in the root, and a task's end with 10,000 objects; if the table pages show, a free entry needs only its generation and its link, so a union brings an entry to 16 bytes.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/fiasco/vm.c` (`erx_fiasco_vm_contiguous_create`) and erXos's kernel: a contiguous object of up to `ERX_MEM_CONTIGUOUS_MAX`, 16 MiB, is zeroed whole at creation in one call, once per device at its first give. On Fiasco that holds the root's service thread for its length; erXos's kernel, which keeps kernel paths bounded, splits it into preemptible steps or moves it to boot. Settled by one 16 MiB create on QEMU `virt` under HVF and on the Pi 4.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/fiasco/vm.c`, `liberx/backend/core/vm_table.c`: a contiguous object is a table of pages like any other, so mapping or revoking a 16 MiB one is 4,096 per-page steps and `l4_task_map` or unmap calls where one flexpage of the run's order could do; the device NIF maps each device's memory once, at open. Settled by mapping and unmapping a 16 MiB contiguous object on QEMU `virt` under HVF.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/fiasco/vm.c` (`erx_fiasco_vm_init`): the contiguous pool is one flexpage of a power of two, so devices declaring 8 MiB and a page take 16 MiB, about 8 MiB the root never uses, and sigma0 must find a block aligned to that size. Settled by the root's free pages on the 112 MiB QEMU `virt` machine with a manifest declaring 8 MiB and a page, against the pool as two or more flexpages of falling order.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/fiasco/vm.c` (`erx_fiasco_vm_physical_create`), `liberx/backend/fiasco/page_pool.c` (`erx_fiasco_device_window_take`): a device window range is placed on a page boundary, and sigma0's library maps 2 MiB flexpages only where the physical base, the size and the root's address are all 2 MiB-aligned (`libsigma0/lib/src/mem.c`, `map_mem`), so QEMU `virt`'s 256 MiB configuration window takes 65,536 sigma0 calls and 4 KiB page tables in the root where 128 would do. Settled by the PCIe domain's start time with the range aligned to 2 MiB when its size is a multiple of it.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/fiasco/libc_start.c` (`erx_fiasco_libc_start`): every musl start, of a runtime, program or test task, makes one more round trip to the root, an `erx_entropy_take` of AT_RANDOM's 16 bytes. Settled by a program's start (`rc` running `true`) and a domain's start with and without the read.
* **[Low] musl, a large getrandom costs one entropy call per 16 bytes.** `getrandom_call` (`musl/src/internal/erxos/syscall.c`) takes 256-byte pieces with `erx_entropy_take`, and the Fiasco backend's `erx_entropy_read` (`liberx/backend/fiasco/service_stubs.c`) asks the root's generator for 16 bytes a reply, so a 1 MiB read makes tens of thousands of round trips and cannot be interrupted. Settled by timing a 1 MiB `getrandom` in the native_musl guest; fix: one request carries a whole piece.
* [Low] `erlang/apps/erx_session/src/erx_session_export.erl` (`random/2`): a read of `/dev/urandom` takes 256 bytes per `erx_entropy_take`, each a round trip to the root's generator on Fiasco, so 64 KiB is 256 calls. Settled by a 64 KiB read against one 256-byte read on QEMU `virt` under HVF.
* [Low] `liberx/system/dom_life.c` (`erx_dom_plan_unit_give`, `erx_dom_unit_check`): a give carries each device's register ranges, about 1.2 KB a give and 10 KB for a creation plan of eight, and the planner keeps a draft give and a window layout on the stack, the check another layout. The system domain's service thread's stack depth for a creation is unmeasured. Settled by that thread's stack high-water mark across a creation of eight gives on QEMU `virt`.
* **[Low] liberx, the second ISB of every clock read.** `erx_fiasco_counter` (`liberx/backend/fiasco/arch/aarch64/clock.c`) reads the counter between two `ISB`s and is never inlined, so every clock read (pipe stamps, `erlang:monotonic_time/0`, timers) pays a second pipeline flush and a call. Settled by loops of 1,000,000 `erx_fiasco_counter` calls and of `erlang:monotonic_time/0` on HVF and the Pi 4, against the parent. Fix: read `CNTVCTSS_EL0` without the first `ISB` where the CPU has `ECV`; Linux's dependent dummy load and `DMB ISHLD` is ruled out.
* **Hosted commits take a host call per host page** (`liberx/backend/hosted/vm.c`, `host_refresh`): one protection per page where a run could be one `mprotect`. Settled by a hosted boot's commit time against a coalesced version.
* [Low] `liberx/backend/hosted/vm.c`, `liberx/backend/core/vm_table.c`: each hosted start clears the range table's 64 MiB index, sized from the 8 GiB pool, though a fresh `mmap` is already zero. Settled by boot time with and without the clear.
* **[Medium] Fiasco backend, a teardown's cost follows reserved addresses, not memory held**
  (`erx_backend_vm_shed_piece`, `liberx/backend/core/vm_table.c`; `erx_fiasco_vm_task_shed`):
  a piece is 512 pages of addresses, each looked up in the page index whether committed or
  not, and Fiasco's unmap visits every entry of a page table that exists. A website domain
  reserves about 272,000 pages, so its teardown is about 530 pieces and 20 to 35 s under TCG
  with `-icount shift=9`, one CPU, and every later stop's exit record waits behind it (probe,
  a throwaway timing of the one-body teardown). Settled by timing a stopped domain's teardown
  and its pieces under HVF and TCG. Fixes: size a piece by committed pages and mappings and
  pass over stretches the index and the mapping records show empty; shrink ERTS's
  reservations.

* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, the one-shot timer's 200 us floor under HVF** ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#scheduling), "The quantum"): Fiasco never programs its timer sooner than 200 us after it last handled its timeout queue (`Timeout_q::program_timer`, `fiasco/kernel/src/kern/timeout.cpp:259-267`), so a deadline or slice end inside the floor is deferred. A throwaway probe gave `wake` `park-deadline` lateness p50 153 us one-shot against 672 us periodic (Mac15,9 host, HVF, 4 vCPUs, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, the `bench` image of revision 1e8ee32d7 with the suite's fast flags, five runs each; the periodic kernel is the baseline), but nothing measures the floor itself. Measure with a probe that stamps each timer interrupt's arrival against the compare value it programmed, and counts how often `program_timer` raises a deadline to the floor, across the `bench` suite's cells; lower `One_shot_min_interval_us` only if the deferrals show in the p99.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, an idle 1 ms `nanosleep` lands either about 254 us or about 505 us late under HVF** ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#deadlines)): the same image gives one mode for a whole boot; the posix row read p50 253.3 us in one boot of 2af8d7a63 and p50 501.3 us in the next, and 501.3 us in two boots with the fix of the timer's compare value and the sub-microsecond spin (Mac15,9, HVF, 1 CPU, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, host load 4 to 9). Every such sleep returned from its first kernel wait, so the second wait is not the cause. With a yielding sibling thread busy on the CPU the same boots read lateness p50 0.1 us (fixed) against 32 us and 199 us (base, where up to 180 of 400 sleeps waited twice), so the extra 250 us is the halted vCPU's wake, not erXos's timer. Settled by: a kernel probe stamping the timer interrupt's arrival against its compare value in both modes, and the same pair of boots of Linux under HVF.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, the timer's compare value costs two divisions** (`erx_clock_first_count`, `kernel/core/clock_inverse.c`, called out of line from `Timer::us_to_ts` on every `Timeout_q::program_timer`), where upstream's conversion was one multiply and shift. Settled by: the `wake` suite's `park-deadline` and the posix rows before and after on the Raspberry Pi 4, where a division costs most.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, the one-shot timer on the Raspberry Pi 4** (`config/fiasco/kernel.defconfig`): the decision to run Fiasco one-shot rests on HVF numbers alone. Run the `bench` suite on the Pi 4 with the periodic and the one-shot kernel, three boots each, and record idle timer interrupts per CPU, idle power, and the paths that wait for an RCU grace period, such as a thread's exit until it is reaped. A CPU with RCU work pending never enters tickless idle: an idle one drives the work at once ([RCU from idle](design-fiasco.md#the-releases-fiasco-build)), and a busy one from its 1 ms RCU tick, which may take two ticks (one to see the new batch, one to report the quiescent state) plus the callbacks, in either timer mode. The floor is lowered only if these show it in the p99.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, a root call behind a task's stop** (`task_unrun`, `liberx/backend/fiasco/service_task.c`; [Task exit](design-fiasco.md#task-exit)): a stop's body waits for one RCU grace period per batch of each stopped task's threads, and then of its semaphores and gate, so every root call of every domain waits behind it. A thread's exit until it is reaped is measured: the `posix` benchmark's `thread-create-join` cell, 10,000 rounds on a Mac15,9 under HVF, QEMU 11.1.3 `virt`, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, the development Fiasco build, went from a median of 6,699 us (p99 8,235 us) at 1 CPU and 6,784 us (p99 8,832 us) at 4 CPUs to 32.1 us (p99 86.0 us) and 35.1 us (p99 65.6 us) once a deletion's objects share one grace period and an idle CPU drives RCU ([RCU from idle](design-fiasco.md#the-releases-fiasco-build)). The latency of a root call made while another domain stops is not measured: time `erx_object_info` from one domain while a second, with many threads on busy CPUs, stops.
* **[Medium] 0.2 on Fiasco, a TLB flush at every idle entry** (`config/fiasco/kernel.defconfig`, `fiasco/kernel/src/kern/context.cpp:2555-2556` and `:2589-2591`): tickless idle flushes the CPU's TLB as it enters and reloads the current space as it leaves, and in one-shot mode Fiasco enters it at every idle moment, however soon the next timeout (`kern/kernel_thread.cpp:383-392`), so each park that idles a CPU pays a flush and a refill. The `bench` suite under HVF showed `vm.protect` p50 31 against 16 counter ticks and `vm.decommit` 33 against 18 after the switch, at a host load of 8.8 against 2.9, so the cause is not settled. Measure the `vm` and `wake` cells with `ONE_SHOT` and `# CONFIG_TICKLESS_IDLE is not set` against tickless idle on an unloaded host and on the Pi 4; keep tickless idle off if the flush shows.
* **[Low] 0.2 on Fiasco, an event at every spin-lock release** (`fiasco/kernel/src/kern/arm/64/spin_lock-arm-64.cpp`, [design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#the-releases-fiasco-build), "The spin lock's event"): each kernel spin-lock release ends with `SEV`, which wakes every CPU waiting in `WFE` for any lock, to re-read its lock and wait again. Settle it with the `bench` suite's IPC and wake cells under HVF and on the Raspberry Pi 4, with and without the `SEV`.

## liberx program layer, pipes and lanes

* **[Low] Pipe core, the writer's wait for the clock to pass its publication.** Every write that published a record (`erx_pipe_write`, the session pipe NIF's `write/2`) performs `ERX_PIPE_OP_PASS_CLOCK` before it returns or waits (`liberx/erx_pipe_run.c`, docs/design-pipes.md "Writing"): two clock reads and a spin of up to one counter tick, about 42 ns at 24 MHz under HVF and 19 ns at the Pi 4's 54 MHz. Settled by 1,000,000 one-byte and 1,000,000 `PIPE_BUF` writes to a reader that keeps up, on HVF and the Pi 4, against the parent, in writes per second. Fallback: weaken PI6 to order only writes more than a tick apart and drop the wait.
* **[Low] Lanes, the first record's ring.** `erx_lane_reader_init` (`liberx/erx_lane.c`) publishes wake 1, so a reader set up before its producer runs is rung at the first record (docs/design-domains.md, the wake handshake); a reader that takes first pays at most one extra ring per lane, at its start. Settled by docs/plan.md's `tools/erx bench pipe`, once it exists: time to a pipe's first byte and throughput, against an init that leaves wake 0 for a reader that takes first.
* **[Low] Program channel, the executor's room announcement takes the channel's lock.** `erx_prog_channel_room` (`liberx/program/channel.c`) locks the channel's mutex for every channel label the event executor takes, may wait while a sender copies a frame of up to the ring's size under it, and wakes each waiting sender with its own `erx_umtx_wake`. Settled by four program threads sending 300-byte frames into a slowly drained ring on HVF, against the parent: frames per second and the executor's label-handling time.
* **[Low] Program channel on Fiasco, a large startup message is paced by the session's band.** A session runs in a band above its programs' (`liberx/backend/fiasco/band.c`), so on a CPU they share, each ring the executor gives the session for room preempts it, and the session refills the 16 KiB inbound ring before the executor runs again: a startup message larger than the ring goes through a switch to the session and back per ring's worth, and the executor never waits on its doorbell (the programs suite's bigargs case, `test/guest/programs/bigargs.c`). Settled by the time from `erx_program:start` to `main` with a 130,000-byte and a 192 KiB argument against a few hundred bytes, on one and four CPUs under HVF; a cost that matters is fixed by the session writing the whole message before it rings, or by a ring larger than a typical startup message.
* **[Low] liberx program layer, the start-up check's scratch.** `erx_prog_startup_plan` (`liberx/program/startup.c`) needs `erx_prog_scratch_bytes(len)`, about four times the startup message, so a message at its 192 KiB bound commits about 786 KiB before `main`, beside the message's own copy. Settled by start-up time and peak commit with a full 192 KiB message against a few hundred bytes; fix: size the scratch from the message's `unique` values.
* [Low] `liberx/program/pipe.c` (`erx_pipe_doorbell`), `liberx/program/fd.c` (`erx_prog_fd_find_end`): each doorbell ring walks the program's live objects under the table lock to find the end. Settled by a ring's time in a program holding 1,000 pipe ends against two.
* [Low] `liberx/program/poll.c`, `erx_prog_poll`: a poll of more than 16 descriptors takes its registrations from `erx_prog_pages_take` and gives them back on every call, a page map and unmap per call (from reading the code). Settled by a loop of 1024-descriptor zero-timeout polls against 16 on QEMU `virt` under HVF; fix: keep the last large poll's pages per thread.
* **[Low] liberx, a page per open file.** `erx_prog_file_openat` (`liberx/program/file.c`) takes each file object as its own run from `erx_prog_pages_take`, a page and two kernel calls per open and one per last close, as each pipe end is. Settled by 1,000 opens and closes of one file against a static pool of `ERX_PROG_FDS_MAX` objects.
* **[Low] liberx, `erx_etf_check` with accept on maps with many or large keys.** `liberx/erx_etf.c` hashes every leaf in a map key with BLAKE3 and each container element with one more call, then heapsorts the key digests; a string key of n bytes costs 2n + 3 hashes. Measure bytes per second with and without accept over maps of 10, 1,000 and 100,000 small keys and over long-string and deep-tuple keys. Fixes: a byte's digest from a table of 256, one pass over a key whose canonical form equals its encoding, or a cheaper keyed hash.

## ERTS and the runtime
* **[Medium] runtime, an interpreted receive walks back to the message it held (edit 78).** `erts_internal:msgq_finish/2` (`otp/erts/emulator/beam/erl_proc_sig_queue.c`) moves the save pointer from the first message to the held one after every match, so an erl_eval receive that scans n non-matching messages costs time quadratic in n. Probe, hosted runtime (emu flavour, native, no QEMU) on an Apple M3 Max, Homebrew clang version 23.1.3, the build's default flags, median of 7 (`~/erxos-scratch/tools/eval_receive_bench.erl`), one interpreted `receive {hit} -> ok end` behind n `{skip, I}` messages, baseline master 3be4d014f: n=10 11.5 to 12.1 us, n=100 36.5 to 33.9 us, n=1000 307 to 844 us, n=10000 3.6 to 82.0 ms. Settles it: that workload as a `tools/erx bench` row, against how long a queue the shell and the system domain's interpreted funs really scan. A scan with a finite timeout also cancels and re-creates its timer for every message it does not take. On the table: keep the link that pointed to the held message and use it while it still does, which needs a proof that every structure change of the queue before the held message (receive markers, priority messages) either clears the link or leaves it valid.

* **[Low] runtime, a dirty hand-off per module load.** Edit 76 of [design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#the-edits-outside-syserxos) moves every `prepare_loading` to a dirty CPU scheduler, so each of a boot's few hundred loads pays a wake of the dirty executor and a park, and back. Settled by `tools/erx bench boot` under HVF on the development image, before and after the edit, several runs each: the cost is nothing to act on when the after median lies within the spread of the before medians.
* **[Low] live, a large load against `erx_code`'s 30 s deadline.** A load into a supervised domain that does not answer within 30 s stops that domain, which loses its running state ([design-live.md](design-live.md#code-from-the-host), "The deadline"), and the 30 s cover the code server's fetch of every piece over the domain's lane and `prepare_loading` and `finish_loading` of the whole batch, up to the 16 MiB `overlay` limit. Settled by a `tools/erx bench` workload, to be written, that loads a 16 MiB batch of new modules into an idle interpreter domain and a BeamAsm domain, on the Raspberry Pi 4 and under TCG on QEMU virt, timed from the load's send to the code server's answer: nothing to act on while the slowest stays well under 30 s; otherwise lower the `overlay` limit or scale the deadline with the batch.
* **[Low] runtime, a dirty hand-off per kernel log record.** `erx_log`'s calls are dirty I/O NIFs ([design-fiasco.md](design-fiasco.md#the-root-service), "The loop"), so every `logger` line `erx_log_h` writes, and every take of the log service, pays a dirty scheduler's wake and park on top of its service round trip, and on erXos's kernel, where the calls do not wait, the hand-off buys nothing; a call that waits behind a long service body is also charged its whole wait in reductions (edit 68 of [design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#the-edits-outside-syserxos)). Settled by a `tools/erx bench` workload of `erx_log:debug/1` in a loop under HVF, with and without the flag, as time per record: nothing to act on while a record costs a small fraction of a millisecond.
* **[Medium] runtime, every domain loads its own kernel and stdlib.** Each ERTS domain's boot script loads `kernel`, `stdlib` and its own applications (258 to 329 modules) into its own memory, and BeamAsm compiles each, so several runtimes starting on one CPU pay it once each. The development image runs storage and network on the interpreter for this reason (`config/manifest/qemu-virt-development.txt`): root's shell came at 5.02 s against 8.26 under TCG and 0.48 against 0.60 under HVF; an image whose shell, block driver and littlefs all run in the system domain reaches it in 1.56 s and 0.21 s (Apple M3 Max, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, 1 guest CPU; ~/erxos-history/boot-fast-2026-11-11 and boot-slow-2026-11-11). Fix: [shared loaded code](design-shared-code.md). Settled by the same timelines, with each domain's "schedulers created" to "kernel up" span, once storage, network, web and session domains adopt it.
* **[Medium] runtime, one AES-128 block per reference.** `erts_sched_make_ref_in_array` (`erl_bif_unique.c`) enciphers a block with ctaes's constant-time software AES for every `make_ref/0`, `gen_server:call` and timer, where stock increments a counter. Settled by `make_ref/0` in a loop and 100,000 local `gen_server:call`s on HVF against the parent; fix: the ARMv8 AES instructions in `erx_ref_permute`.
* **[Low] runtime, one AES decipher per timer cancel or read.** `erts_get_ref_numbers_thr_id` deciphers a timer's reference to find its scheduler on every `erlang:cancel_timer/1,2` and `read_timer/1,2`. Settled by `start_timer`/`cancel_timer` pairs on HVF against the parent.
* **[Medium] runtime, the alias registry on every `gen_server:call`.** Each call's alias monitor inserts into `erx_alias.c`'s striped table under a mutex and removes at the reply, and every local reference that `binary_to_term/1` or a frame decodes looks the registry up, then the magic table. Settled by 100,000 local `gen_server:call`s on 1 and 4 schedulers on HVF against the parent, with stripe-lock contention from `lcnt`.
* **[Low] ERTS, two clock reads per dirty I/O call.** `erts_dirty_process_main` (`otp/erts/emulator/beam/beam_common.c`) charges every dirty call its elapsed time (edit 68 of [design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#the-edits-outside-syserxos)), two `erx_clock_ns` reads per call, a short `prim_file` read included. Settled by `erts_debug:dirty_io(scheduler, type)` and small `file:read/2` calls in a loop, with and without the charge. Fix if it shows: take the hand-back read from the scheduler's cached `last_monotonic_time`.
* **[Low] runtime, the literal allocator keeps ERTS's carrier sizes.** `+Mu` does not reach `literal_alloc` or `temp_alloc` (`aui[]` in `erl_alloc.c`'s `handle_args`), so an interpreter domain at rest holds 2.0 MiB of literal and 0.25 MiB of temporary carriers whatever `erx_boot_argv_plan` says (`Footprint` in `tools/lib/erx/medium_boot.rb`). Measure pool pages with `+MIlmbcs 1024 +MIsmbcs 128` (and `+MImmbcs` lowered) on QEMU `virt` under HVF; add the flags if they save memory.
* **[Medium] runtime, a cache of two carriers may slow decoding and scanning.** Every runtime starts with `+MMmcs 2` (`erx_boot_argv_plan`; docs/design-runtime.md, "Memory"). On a loaded host, G5's BeamAsm `json_decode` took 12.1 to 13.6 ms against 7.5 to 8.7 with five or ten carriers, and `scan` 62.7 to 66.7 against 49.8 to 55.3 (`tools/erx bench g5 --accel hvf`, Apple M3 Max, QEMU 11.1.3; ~/erxos-history/alloc-flags-2026-11-10/); five carriers cost the system domain about 670 pool pages and a network domain about 320. Settled by G5 at `+MMmcs 2`, `3` and `5` on an unloaded host, ten samples per domain, weighing time against pages.
* **[Low] runtime, the gate worker triggers under its lock.** The gate worker (`sys/erxos/erx_gate.c`) holds `gate_lock` across `erx_irq_trigger`, on Fiasco a request to the root, so a scheduler registering, taking or unregistering a port's records waits behind it. Measure the take's latency in the `programs` suite with many programs ending at once; fix: a per-sink generation that lets the trigger run outside the lock.
* **[Low] runtime, a program's image found by walking the release.** The erx_program driver's start (`sys/erxos/erx_program_drv.c`, `release_index`) reads the session release's entries one by one with `erx_release_entry`, copying each path, under the port's lock. Measure start latency in the `programs` suite against the release's file count; fix: share `erx_prim_file.c`'s sorted name table (`erx_release_fs_lookup`), or binary-search the sorted entries.
* **`read_file` copies from the release's pages.** `erx_prim_file.c` serves `prim_file:read_file/1` through stock's `read_file` (`prim_file_nif.c`), which fills a new binary, where the design allows a binary over the slice's pages. Settled by a boot that loads every module of `kernel` and `stdlib` from an archive, with and without the copy.
* **[Low] runtime, first-open hash of release files on the target.** `erx_prim_file.c` hashes each release file at its first open with BLAKE3's portable code, so a boot hashes every module it loads; small hosted, unmeasured on the target. Settled by boot to `1>` with and without the check under TCG, HVF and on the Pi 4.
* **[Low] runtime, dirty-scheduler switches before a release path.** A relative release path calls `get_cwd_nif` and then `release_walk_nif`, both dirty I/O, before the operation's NIF, and `efile_path_t` puts about 4 KiB on the C stack, two for a rename or link. Measure `file:read_file_info/1` on a relative against an absolute release path, and check the dirty-I/O thread stack size.
* **`erts_mremap` grows by copying** (`otp/erts/emulator/sys/common/erl_mmap.c`, `remap_move`): with no `mremap` on either target, a growing carrier takes new pages and copies, where stock on Linux moves the mappings. Settled by counting `mseg_realloc` calls and bytes in an allocation-heavy OTP suite natively.
* [Low] `otp/erts/emulator/sys/erxos/erx_event.c`, `beam/erl_process.c`: a wake through the ready list against stock's poll thread and scheduler wake. Settled by a bench of an IRQ object's trigger to a process sink's receive, hosted, against stock's `enif_select` on a pipe, once the pipe NIF or the device NIF gives it a consumer.
* [Low] `otp/erts/emulator/sys/erxos/erx_event.c`: `deliver` allocates one short-lived environment per delivery to a process sink, on top of the message. Settled by the same bench at a driver's interrupt rate, with and without one environment kept per entry.
* **A module load under BeamAsm on four CPUs is slow.** In `native_domain`'s `jit_reload` case (HVF, 4 CPUs) a round of soft purge, load and every scheduler's call takes a few hundred milliseconds, far more than on one CPU, so the case runs 40 rounds, not 200. Suspects: the code barrier's aux work and the thread-progress ticks a load and purge wait for. Settled by loading and purging a small module in a loop at 1, 2 and 4 CPUs under the interpreter and BeamAsm images.

* **[Medium] lanes, a bell's hop to the lane port's input task** (`otp/erts/emulator/sys/erxos/erx_lane_drv.c`): a bell schedules the port's input task instead of draining at the dispatching executor, the hop stock ERTS pays from the poll thread to a TCP distribution port. Settled by the time from the peer's bell to `erts_net_message` on HVF, with one scheduler and with every `+S` scheduler busy.
* **[Low] lanes, the control table's scan on every run** ([design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#connections-in-erts)): each run of a domain's input task on its system connection loads every control slot's `seq`, 256 words at the default `connections`, though a record arrives a few times per connection's life. Settled by bell to `erts_net_message` for small frames on HVF at `connections` 1 and 4,096; fix: a table-wide count of records written, loaded once per run.
* [Low] `otp/erts/emulator/sys/erxos/erx_exports.c`, `erx_dist_exports_1`: each holder of a name is checked against its peer's connection, a distribution-table read and a walk of its entries, uncharged, so a widely handed name costs holders times connections per call. Settled by `erx_dist:exports/1` on a name with 1,000 holders across 64 connections.
* [Low] `otp/erts/preloaded/src/init.erl` (`report_ready/0`), `erlang/apps/erx_domain/src/erx_domain_srv.erl` (`grant_facets/2`): after its ready report and before `-eval`, `-s` and `-run`, a domain waits for the domain service to mint its facets, one round trip plus a facet start per facet. Settled by domain creation to the first `-eval` with no facets and with several.
* **[Low] domains, a session's start has no bench.** `erx_domain_srv:start_session/3` checks the start (a scan of the template's grants in `erx_domain_mints:granted/3`), names the session (`erx_domain_core:session_prefix/3`) and builds it; no latency change is expected, but nothing measures it. Settled by timing `erx_domain:start_session/2` over many starts and stops of root's session in the session_domain image, against building a configured domain of the same release.
* [Low] the domain service's `svc` grants ([design-app-trees.md](design-app-trees.md#granting)): every session whose template holds `start` is introduced to every running app declaring `serves svc`, one connection each, carved from both outer budgets, whether or not anyone reads the tree. Measure, on the Pi 4 image with ntpd and a second tree-serving app, the memory and time a connection adds to a root login; fix: grant at the first walk of `/svc/NAME` ([design-app-trees.md](design-app-trees.md#decisions), 4).
* **[Medium] inspection, records crossing the boundary.** Every inspection crosses to the system domain and, for runtime attributes, on to the domain's agent and back ([design-live.md](design-live.md#the-inspect-service)): `erx:i/1`, each `/proc` open and each browser view cost two round trips, and a listing page is cut at an unmeasured `inspect_reply_max` of 256 KiB. A host probe (OTP 29.1.2 JIT, Apple M3 Max) of a worker holding one 81,000-word answer of 1,000 records peaked at 833,000 words, which sets `inspect_worker_heap` at 16 MiB and 32 MiB reserved per holder; the frame floor raised system and session `max_frame` to 512 KiB, keeping the funnel's scratch at twice the largest frame. Settled by `tools/erx bench inspect` on QEMU: record and `[processes]` page sizes at 100 and 1,000 processes, the workers' peak heap at scope `all`, `cat /proc/status` and `erx:i([processes, Pid])` latency from a session against the system shell, and a session's peak footprint. Fix: an agent answer carrying a page of records.
* **[Low] inspection, a `/proc` directory listed by one query per literal child.** `erx_procfs_core:listing/3` (`below/3`) asks one `{exists, Path}` per schema-declared child of `/proc` and `/proc/domains/N` ([design-live.md](design-live.md#the-proc-tree)), each a round trip to a query worker (and on to D's agent below `[domains, D]`), at most the grant's `in_flight` at once. Settled by `ls /proc` and `ls /proc/domains/N` from root's session against the system shell; fix: one query answering which declared children the scope reaches.
* **[Medium] inspection, a `/proc` walk below `/proc/processes` collects the whole listing.** Each walk step asks `{exists, Path}` (`erx_procfs_core:walk/3`), and the step to `/proc/processes` collects every domain's processes from every agent, so `cat /proc/processes/*/status` costs O(N^2) records for N files. Settled by that command at 100 and 1,000 processes. Fixes: an existence query answered without collecting a listing, or a walk that skips the question for a schema-declared directory whose child it asks anyway.
* **[Low] inspection, every collection of a query worker is a major one.** `erx_inspect_worker` spawns workers with `{fullsweep_after, 0}` (`spawn_options/0`) to stay within `inspect_worker_heap` ([design-live.md](design-live.md#the-inspect-service), "The query worker"), so each collection copies the whole page. Measure a `[processes]` listing at scope `all` over 1,000 processes with and without the option, reductions and peak heap; keep it if the peak without it passes the bound or the cost is small.
* **[Low] runtime, one open of `/proc/kernel/log`.** `erx_log_records:record/2` reads about four records per `erx_log:read/1`, and each read walks the ring's headers from the oldest record (`kernel/core/log.c`, `erx_log_copy`), so a full ring costs hundreds of walking reads in the `/proc` grant's process, which holds its other requests meanwhile. Measure opening a full 64 KiB ring on QEMU `virt` under HVF; fixes: a larger window per read, a cursor the copy can seek to.
* **[Medium] domains, the network client in every domain's base.** Every domain boot script starts `erx_net_client` (`ReleaseFiles::DOMAIN_BASE`), which loads `erx_net_client_app`, `erx_net_client_sup` and `erx_inet` (about 10 KB of BEAM) and adds its application master and supervisor; domains boot in interactive mode, so nothing else of it loads. No domain's memory is measured with it: the storage domain's fixed 30 MiB (7,680 pages) holds a measured 7,635, about 180 KiB of headroom (`config/manifest/qemu-virt-development.txt`), and the Footprint table there labels the bare release as the four applications without it. Settled by the block_device suite's storage measurement and the Footprint cell (`tools/lib/erx/medium_boot.rb`) re-run, with the manifest's figures and labels updated from them.

## Drivers and devices

* **[Low] console, one input message per byte while the mini UART echoes.** `erx_mini_uart`'s send drains the receive FIFO whenever its read of LSR before a byte shows one (`send_step/1`), so during an echo each drain usually finds one byte and the console receives one `erx_console_input` per byte, answered by a one-byte `erx_console_output`, where a drain at the interrupt took up to eight; each such drain also scans the driver's mailbox once for a waiting delivery (`drain_in_send/1`). Settled by counting messages and the console process's reductions per pasted line on the Raspberry Pi 4, natively, with and without the drain in the send.
* **[Low] drivers, the cost of a register access from Erlang.** `erx_device:read/2` and `write/3` are BIFs that resolve and check a register's name in the generated table, unmeasured against the access itself (a VM exit under HVF, an uncached load on hardware) or the interrupt-to-process wake. Measure register reads on HVF and a Pi, split into call, lookup, access and wake. Fixes, cheapest first, behind `erx_device`: a batch call, names resolved at the driver's start to an index, a loader-specialized instruction that does the access inline.
* **[Low] devices, the DMA ledger's size and scans.** `erx_dma_ledger_t` (`otp/erts/emulator/nifs/erxos/erx_device_ledger.h`) holds 256 holdings of 64 bytes, about 16.6 KB, and its accessors scan every live holding, so a sequencer that returns the next ledger by value copies it per decision and the room rule scans the holdings once per table. Settled by the block and network throughput workloads over the split ring once the sequencer drives it, under HVF and on a Pi 4, against a ledger updated in place with a per-record held map.
* **[Low] drivers, a full barrier after every register store.** `store()` in `otp/erts/emulator/nifs/erxos/erx_device.c` waits for each store (`dsb sy`) so a bus error is taken in its caller; GENET's descriptors are registers, so each frame sent or buffer returned costs several waits. Measure the Pi 4's frame rate over erx_genet with and without it, natively; drop it from the ring paths if it shows.
* **[Low] The device access trace on every register access in development builds** (`erx_device.c`, [design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#the-device-access-trace)): each access takes the domain's trace lock across the access and makes five stores to the crash area, and a domain's schedulers take turns at their devices' registers. Settled by the network throughput workload on a development build with the trace on and off, under HVF and natively on a Pi 4: it stays on if its cost lies within the runs' spread, otherwise it moves under a boot word. `erx.devsync=1`'s store barrier is a debugging mode and is not measured.
* **[Medium] device NIF, zeroing a whole buffer per post, coherent devices too.** The sequencer zeroes each posted buffer outside the bytes the device reads ([design-device-dma.md](design-device-dma.md#the-construction-handoffs-of-granules), rule 1), so a small request in a large buffer (a 16-byte block header in 64 KiB) zeroes nearly all of it, on QEMU `virt` too. Settled by block reads and network transmits against zeroing cut to the writable segments; fix: buffers sized to each queue's requests.
* **[Medium] device NIF, cache maintenance per buffer on a device that is not coherent.** Every post zeroes the bytes no `out` fills and cleans each line handed over, and every completion cleans and invalidates each returning line and waits on a full-system barrier ([design-device-dma.md](design-device-dma.md#the-construction-handoffs-of-granules)): for a 1,536-byte GENET receive buffer, a 1,536-byte fill, 24 line operations of 64 bytes and a barrier per call; every register write also takes the device lock. Settled on the Pi 4 by GENET traffic at line rate against the same with maintenance and zeroing skipped (wrong bytes, cost alone), and `post/4` and register-write microbenchmarks with and without the lock. Fixes: more completions per call sharing a barrier; a lock per device, not per NIF.
* **[Low] drivers, a shared interrupt line adds two messages per driver per firing.** A line two devices of a unit name reaches its drivers through its line owner (`erx_irq_line`; [design-shared-devices.md](design-shared-devices.md#performance)), which notifies each driver, waits for every answer and then acknowledges, so each driver pays two messages and the line stays masked until the slowest answers. Settled by two stand-in devices on one soft line, hosted and under HVF on QEMU `virt`: trigger to driver and to acknowledgement, against a stand-in bound directly, at 1 and 10,000 triggers a second. Fix: fan-out in the device NIF, at the price of a new sink kind.
* **The PL011 console driver sends a byte at a time** (`erlang/apps/erx_uart/src/erx_pl011.erl`, `send`): two register BIFs per byte (read `FR.TXFF`, write `DR`), and a full FIFO waits a whole millisecond. `cat /NOTICES` reached the host's terminal at about 256 KB/s (769,610 bytes in 3 s; Apple M3 Max, HVF, QEMU 11.1.3, one CPU, serial on a pseudo-terminal), a minute for 16 MB. Settled by the console transcript's 20,000-byte output under HVF against a send that waits for `FR.TXFE` and writes a whole FIFO without reading `FR`.
* **[Low] Bluetooth, `erx_pl011`'s transmit wait.** The line service's driver waits 1 ms at a time while its transmit FIFO is full (`erlang/apps/erx_uart/src/erx_pl011.erl:21`, `:45`, `:236-243`; [design-bluetooth.md](design-bluetooth.md#the-controllers-line)), which the patch download and notification bursts hit. Settled by timing the patch download with the wait and with a transmit-space interrupt; fix: the PL011's transmit interrupt.
* **[Low] Bluetooth, the patch download at 115200 baud.** The Pi 4's bring-up replays `BCM4345C0.hcd`, 323 records and 64,129 bytes on the line, one HCI command and Command Complete per record at 115200 baud, waiting 1 s after the minidriver and 4 s after the launch ([design-bluetooth.md](design-bluetooth.md#the-bring-up)), so advertising starts some eleven seconds after bring-up. Settled by timing `reset` to advertising on the board, and the download with Zephyr's shorter waits (probe B-P5). Fixes: vendor rate command `0xFC18` with a `rate` word in the line's `ctl`; the shorter waits.
* **[Low] Bluetooth, a phone's write across two domains.** A write request reaches its handler in an app's domain through the Bluetooth domain's link, the registry and the `ble` facet, and the link takes the next request only after the answer ([design-bluetooth.md](design-bluetooth.md#the-ble-facet)), so the round trip bounds a phone's write rate. Settled by a peer script timing writes through an echo handler, on QEMU and on the Pi 4, against writes answered inside the Bluetooth domain; fix: a handler that runs in the Bluetooth domain.
* **[Medium] WiFi, programmed I/O, spaced register writes and two domain crossings per frame.** Every byte to and from the Pi 4's CYW43455 passes the legacy SD controller's data port as 32-bit accesses in the storage domain's `erx_sdio_host`, on a 4-bit 25 MHz bus (12.5 MB/s at most); non-data register writes are spaced 6 us, two per command; the card interrupt is polled every 1 ms; and each received frame waits up to a poll period, then costs a transaction-list round trip between storage and WiFi domains and a message to the network domain ([design-wifi.md](design-wifi.md#performance)). Settled by `tools/erx bench wifi`, to be written, on the board: firmware load time, TCP throughput both ways and UDP request-reply latency to a FreeBSD peer, CPU per MB in each domain, idle storage-domain CPU, with GENET as baseline; and time from a lost link to an open port, and throughput on a dual-band access point. Fixes, cheapest first: a tuned poll period, 50 MHz high speed, glom on transmit, a C bulk copy of the data port, the SoC's DMA on DREQ 11 (FreeBSD `bcm2835_sdhci.c:126`), the chip process in the network domain; for the station, scanning the channels last joined and a band preference.
* **[Low] board sensors, a firmware request per reading.** On the Pi 4 `erx_board_sensors` makes nine property requests every 5 s, each holding the device service for a kernel call that busy-polls the mailbox ([design-board-sensors.md](design-board-sensors.md#performance)). Settled by `poll_duration` in `/proc/sensors/status`, idle and under load. Fixes: a longer period; one buffer carrying every tag of a poll.
* **[Low] usb, one controller process for every transfer.** Every xHCI transfer passes the one controller process and the TRB ring form's completion, one NIF call per batch of at most 32 events ([design-usb.md](design-usb.md#the-trb-ring-form)). Settled by mass-storage throughput at 64 sectors a request on QEMU `virt`'s `qemu-xhci` against FreeBSD 15.1 on the same device, under HVF, with a keyboard and tablet reporting. Fixes: larger bulk buffers, completions routed to pipes without the process's step.
* **[Medium] usb, a key's latency to the router.** A USB key passes the controller process, the HID function, its input number's stream, the file protocol and the display's reader before the router ([design-usb.md](design-usb.md#hid-reaches-the-display)). Settled by timing QMP key events to the router's step on QEMU `virt` under HVF and on the Pi 4, against virtio-input's path; fix if the file protocol dominates: the display reads a local stream when the controller is placed in the display domain.
* **[Low] usb, `erx_hid_core:step/3` per report.** The fold looks up each value in its field's usage ranges, so a worst-case 1 KiB layout makes a report cost values times ranges. Settled by folding reports against a worst-case layout and the boot keyboard's; fix: classify each value's usage once when the layout is parsed.

* **[Low] The TRB form's answer array.** `erx_trb_form_complete` takes an answer array of `ERX_TRB_FORM_ANSWERS_MAX(count)`, the events plus `ERX_DMA_HOLDINGS_MAX` less one for the transfers one command's success may retire, about 13 KiB of 48-byte answers for a 32-event call, on the stack of the NIF thread that calls it once `erx_device.c` does. Settled by measuring that call path's stack depth once `erx_device.c` calls it; if it matters, a bound from the TRB form's own rings replaces the ledger's.

## Storage and file systems

* **The block driver's request geometry.** `erx_blk` posts three segments per request (header, sectors, status), so its 16-descriptor queue holds five requests while the pool reserves sixteen 4113-byte buffers whose odd stride shares cache lines. Two segments and a cache-line-rounded buffer would double the requests in flight. Settled by read throughput under HVF.
* **One request in flight per window.** `erx_blk_window` answers one request at a time and waits on `erx_blk` for each, so a file system on one window keeps one request at the device. Settled by read throughput under HVF through one window against several clients of the device process.
* **[Medium] storage, a drive's window proxy and its share** (`erx_blk_drive_proxy`, [design-drives.md](design-drives.md#how-the-grant-reaches-the-media-domain)): every sector request of the media domain takes one local hop more, through the proxy, and the proxy's work is charged to a share of 1,000,000 reductions a second, a figure chosen without a measurement, which may cap a stick's read rate below the transport's. Settled by a large file's read throughput through `/media` on a QEMU USB stick under HVF, with and without the proxy, and the reductions each request costs the proxy.
* **[Medium] storage, the FAT server's chain walks** (`erx_fat`, [design-storage.md](design-storage.md#removable-media-fat)): a read walks its file's chain from the first cluster each time, reading one FAT sector per link through the window, with no cache of FAT sectors or of the last position, and a lookup reads its whole directory; reading a large file in 9P-sized pieces is quadratic in its clusters, and each read holds a list and a map of every cluster up to its end, tens of MiB near the end of a 4 GiB file of 4 KiB clusters, which may pass the volume's heap bound. Settled by a large file's sequential read through `/media` on a QEMU USB stick under HVF, against the same file's raw sectors through the drive's proxy. Fixes: one cached FAT sector per walk, and each open file's last cluster and its index.
* **[Medium] storage, five flushes per FAT batch and the batch bound** (`erx_fat`, [design-storage.md](design-storage.md#removable-media-fat)): every batch flushes after each of its five steps that has writes, three for a copy that only appends, and a copy that never syncs commits a batch each time its data passes a quarter of `media_volume_bytes`, 512 KiB, so a large copy to a stick pays five device flushes per 512 KiB. A USB stick's SYNCHRONIZE CACHE latency is unmeasured; if it is tens of milliseconds, the flushes, not the bus, bound a copy's throughput. Measure a `tools/erx bench` copy of 256 MiB to a FAT32 stick on the Pi 4 against the same copy with flushes skipped (a throwaway hack, not a mode). Fixes on the table: a larger bound for the batch's data where the volume's memory allows,.
* **[Medium] storage, the SD controller's programmed I/O in Erlang.** `erx_blk_sd` ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#the-sd-host-controller)) moves each 512-byte block as 128 data-port accesses, each a device-NIF BIF, takes one interrupt per block through the line's owner, and spaces other writes at least 6 us, all on the device process's core; the bus carries 12.5 MB/s at 25 MHz, 25 at 50 MHz. Settled by `tools/erx bench blk` on the Pi 4 at default and high speed, reads and writes of 8 and 128 sectors. Fixes: a C bulk copy of the data port in the device NIF (a measured row of the driver table), and DMA, whose design is pending.
* **[Medium] storage, a device flush per littlefs commit** (`erx_lfs`, `erx_blk_virtio`, [design-storage.md](design-storage.md#read-write-storage)): littlefs calls `bd_sync` (`otp/erts/emulator/nifs/erxos/erx_littlefs_nif.c:300`) at every metadata commit, possibly more than once (`lfs_dir_commitcrc`, `littlefs/lfs.c:1773`), and before the commit of a non-inline file's sync (`littlefs/lfs.c:3443-3446`); each becomes `VIRTIO_BLK_T_FLUSH`, on QEMU a flush of the host's image file. Every create, rename, removal and attribute change pays at least one, an `fsync` at least two. Settled by 1,000 small files created, written and `fsync`ed on `/data` under HVF, against a tree that answers the sync at once. No fix may drop a flush littlefs asks for; the fix is fewer commits per operation in `erx_lfs`.
* **[Medium] storage, F2FS's two flushes and pre-image per `fsync` record** ([design-f2fs.md](design-f2fs.md#fsync)): a record flushes before and after its commit block and writes that block twice, first as its pre-image, where Linux writes it once with preflush and FUA. Settled by `tools/erx bench fs` `fsync` latency on QEMU under HVF and the Pi 4's SD card, against littlefs and Linux's F2FS in the oracle guest. Fixes: group commit across waiting `fsync`s, a forced-unit-access write in the block service.
* **[Medium] storage, F2FS writes without a buffer** ([design-f2fs.md](design-f2fs.md#operations)): a write shorter than a block rewrites the whole block at a new address, so short appends rewrite their last block each time, and every block request passes the one volume process. Settled by `tools/erx bench fs` appends of 100 bytes and 64 KiB and sequential reads, against littlefs. Fixes: a per-file write buffer drained by every checkpoint and `fsync`; window requests larger than a block.
* **[Medium] storage, F2FS runs one request of the volume at a time** ([design-f2fs.md](design-f2fs.md#processes-and-ownership)): a client waits behind another's long request on a different file, and the block driver sees one request's blocks in flight. Settled by two grants, one streaming 64 KiB reads and one appending to another file, on QEMU under HVF and the Pi 4's SD card, each client's throughput and worst latency against either alone. Fix: run requests on different files at once, ordered per file.
* **[Low] storage, F2FS checkpoints without journals, the 512-block charge, and mount time** ([design-f2fs.md](design-f2fs.md#checkpoints)): every checkpoint writes whole NAT and SIT blocks per changed entry, runs whenever the epoch's charge reaches 512 blocks, and a mount reads the whole SIT, node segment footers when the pack lacks node summaries, and the chain, rewriting every replayed node. Settled by blocks written and checkpoints per second over the file-semantics corpus and an untar of many small files, and mount time of an 8 GiB volume empty, full and with a long chain. Fixes: NAT and SIT journals, node summaries in every pack, a charge bound and room reserve that grow with the volume.
* **[Low] storage, F2FS's greedy cleaner and its overprovisioning** ([design-f2fs.md](design-f2fs.md#the-cleaner)): background cleaning takes the emptiest segment, not the oldest, and a fragmented volume dirties a dnode per moved block. Settled by G13's churn to 90 % utilization, blocks moved per block written, at every format size. Fixes: cost-benefit choice in the background, larger overprovisioning.
* **[Low] files, reading an EROFS image without a cache** (`erx_erofs`, `erx_verity`, [design-storage.md](design-storage.md#reading-erofs-images)): every operation reads its names' inode and directory blocks through the funnel again, the superblock's CRC-32C runs bit by bit in Erlang at each mount, an open file (`erx_erofs_file`, `erx_storage_system`) walks its names again per read, the `system` tree's directory open resolves each entry's path from the root, and `erx_verity` sends one window request per 4 KiB block. Settled by operations a second over a release image on the host's stock OTP with and without the design's metadata cache; fixes: that cache, and an open file keeping its inode.
* **[Low] storage, the `system` tree's mount at boot** (`erx_fs_mint`, `erx_storage_system:mount/1`, [design-storage.md](design-storage.md#the-system-tree)): the mint mounts the tree after its start, so the storage domain's ready report does not wait, but the first grant of the tree does: superblock, root and `apps/` read through `erx_verity`, each block a window round trip and a SHA-256 path, and the system domain's first `system` grant waits in the mint's queue, against `erx_domain_mints`' 10 s deadline, after which the pair stays failed for the boot. Settled by the time from the mint's start to its mount's end and to the first answered `system` grant, on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4, with a release system partition.
* **[Medium] storage, the kiosk's first frame waits on the `system` tree.** An image package lies in the slot's system partition, so its app starts only once the block driver and the storage domain serve the `system` tree ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#the-system-tree)). Measure boot to the kiosk's first frame on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4, against a build that carries the dashboard in the boot image. Fix: start the storage domain earlier; nothing that puts a package back in the boot image.
* **[Medium] storage, a package read through the `system` grant.** A 64 KiB read of an image package crosses to the storage domain, which reads its window and serves the blocks over the grant, where a release slice's read is a NIF call over mapped pages ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#reading-erofs-images)). Measure `tools/erx bench fs` 64 KiB reads through the grant against the release slice, and Doom's 34 MiB package load from its mount, on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4. Fixes: larger reads per `erx_verity` call, read-ahead in the backend.
* **[Medium] storage, the boot image stays in RAM for the whole boot.** The development Pi 4 boot image is 68 MB ([TODO.md](../TODO.md), storage and images), and every domain's and session template's release lies in it ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#data-model)). Measure, on the Pi 4 card's image, each archive entry's bytes grouped by application, the system domain's library tree against the domains', and the releases' share of `root_memory` on the production and kiosk images; then free RAM after boot with the duplicate OTP files and the second OpenSSL removed. Fixes if it crowds out domains: deduplicate shared files and link OpenSSL once, or move a release to the system partition as a self-checking file once a design names its reader and consumer.
* **[Low] storage, SHA-256 of each 4 KiB block `erx_verity` checks.** Every block a domain reads from a package is hashed in that domain in dm-verity's tree format ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#reading-erofs-images)), and the Pi 4's Cortex-A72 lacks the SHA-2 extension, so it hashes in plain C. Measure `erx_verity:check/3`'s cost per block on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4, `tools/erx bench fs` on the Pi 4, and Doom's load time with the check on and off. Fixes: the tree's blocks cached across reads; larger reads; BLAKE3, already in the tree, if worth giving up dm-verity's format.
* **[Low] storage, two SHA-256s for each package block read through the `system` tree.** The storage domain checks every system-partition block against the partition's root hash and the client checks it again against its package's tree ([design-storage.md](design-storage.md#the-system-tree)), twice where an installed package's block is hashed once. Measure the same 64 KiB reads and Doom's load from an image package and from the same package installed, on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4. Fix: a larger cache of checked blocks in the backend; none that drops either check.
* **[Low] shell, `mount` counts every block of /data to learn its type.** `erx_cmd_mount` sends each grant a 9P statfs for its `type`, and `erx_lfs` answers with `lfs_fs_size`, which traverses every block in use while other `/data` requests queue; the request has no deadline, so a stalled card makes `mount` wait until Ctrl-C (FreeBSD's `mount` uses `MNT_NOWAIT`, `sbin/mount/mount.c:296-297`). Measure `time mount` on a `/data` of 10 and 10,000 files on the Pi 4. Fixes: `erx_lfs` answers statfs from kept counts, or the grant reports its type without asking the volume.
* **`/tmp`'s hops per operation** (`erx_ramfs`, [design-tmp.md](design-tmp.md#where-tmp-runs)): an operation is two local round trips (the grant, then the tree process) plus the NIF's resolve for a path, where a C provider would cost one dirty-scheduler move; Common Test writes its logs a line at a time. Measure 10,000 line-sized `file:write/2` and 1,000 `file:read_file_info/1` and `file:read_file/1` of 4 KiB files on `/tmp`, against the release file system and `erx_ramfs_core:step/4` alone, and hosted against stock OTP 29.1.2; also the tree process's memory against `held` ([Charges](design-tmp.md#data-model)). Fixes: the grant calling the tree directly for a file with one open; shape (b) of the design, one round trip.
* **[Low] files, resolving a path in Erlang** (`erx_path`, [design-files.md](design-files.md#resolving-a-path)): each run is list work over up to 256 names and 64 entries in the caller; a `.` or `..` after an unwalked name costs one more walk (and one more `release_walk_nif` on the release), a link two round trips; and every run retraces from the entry's top, so a 4 KiB path of 255 names, some 700 `/b/..` repeats and 32 such link texts walks millions of names where FreeBSD looks up tens of thousands. Measure hosted against stock OTP 29.1.2: 1,000 `file:read_file_info/1` of `/tmp/a/b/c`, `/tmp/a/x/../b/c` and a path through a link, and the same on the release, at 4 and 64 entries; and that worst path's walk count. Fixes: keep the table sorted by point length; send a `..` after a new name in the same walk, answered from the fid's record (design-files.md's Alternatives); walk on from the last fid when the place only grew.
* **[Medium] programs, an `open` builds its namespace view and its file anew** (`erx_file_description`, `prim_file:erx_open_at/5`, `erx_file_remote:described/3`; [design-programs.md](design-programs.md#descriptors-and-files)): each open of a native program spawns a description that maps the whole narrowed table through the facet registry (`erx_facets:table/1`), resolves the path from scratch (`erx_path:resolve/6`) and makes a fresh `atomics` array and `prim_buffer` for a file whose description never reads a line and, for a program, reads nothing ahead. On master 212e48ff7, `tools/erx bench posix`'s `open` row was 50.7 us p50; with throwaway hacks that skipped the handler's frame checks, memoized the table and the resolution and reused one buffer, 44.0 us; with only the handler's reply checks removed for real, 49.3 us, so the table, the resolution and the buffer together cost about 5 us (one boot each, one CPU, HVF on an Apple M3 Max, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, landing profile). A hosted probe on stock OTP 29.1.2 (emu flavour, native) puts the table of 12 entries at 0.37 us, the resolution of a three-name path at 1.1 us and `prim_buffer:new/0` at 0.25 us, so the guest pays several times the hosted cost. Settles it: the row with one of the three removed at a time. Fixes: map through the registry only the entry the resolution lands on; make the buffer when a read first needs one.
* **A grant's step count over a whole fid path** (`erx_fs_grant:repath/3`, [design-files.md](design-files.md#serving-the-erx_fs-behaviour)): every new fid, walk in place and clunk counts or uncounts each step of the fid's path, so a clone at depth D costs D map updates and an owner's end costs the sum of its fids' depths. Measure a clone and a clunk at depths 1, 16 and 256, against counting only the steps that differ; fix: count steps per shared path suffix so a clone is one increment.
* **[Low] files, a scan of every process per namespace change** (`erx_ns`, [design-files.md](design-files.md#building-binding-and-mounting)): the table is a persistent term, so each `mount`, `bind` and `unmount` replaces it, and a replaced non-immediate value goes through `erts_queue_release_literals` (`otp/erts/emulator/beam/erl_bif_persistent.c:1189-1203`, :1293-1300), whose collector makes every process search its heap (`beam_bif_load.c:1466-1490`); the working directory stays out of a persistent term for this reason. Measure, hosted, 1,000 `erx_ns:bind/2` and `unmount/1` pairs with 100 and 10,000 idle 1 MiB processes, and a domain's start with and without its usual mounts. Fix: batch a domain's start-up mounts into one put.

## Network

* **[Medium] network, the Raspberry Pi 5's Ethernet crosses two domains** ([design-rp1.md](design-rp1.md#where-it-runs-and-where-its-frames-go)). RP1's GEM is driven in the `rp1` domain and lent to the network domain through `ether`, so every frame and every credit return is a message between domains, and the GEM ring form holds a transmit post that falls in a group still handed until the group completes. Measure TCP bulk throughput and UDP echo latency on `cgem0` against line rate and against Linux on the same board (the GN-board workloads). Fixes on the table: larger `ether` batches; encoding frames straight into the lane; giving RP1 to the network domain, which property 9 refuses in a release build.
* **[Medium] network, the Pi 4 network domain's memory is not measured.** `config/manifest/development.txt` gives the network domain QEMU virt's 36 MiB, measured over `erx_virtio_net`; over `erx_genet`, whose two queues hold 256 buffers of 1,536 bytes each, it is unmeasured. Measure the domain's pool pages on the board, as the net_device suite's network-domain cell does on QEMU, after a DHCP lease, `ping -c 3` and a 1 MiB TCP echo, and size the row from it.
* **[Medium] network, one connection process per flow as a ceiling** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#one-owner-one-step)). Every segment of a connection is stepped in one process, so a flow gets at most one scheduler. On the FreeBSD x86-64 test host (FreeBSD 16.0-CURRENT, Threadripper PRO 5995WX, stock OTP 29, erx_net 6de4bb62 with the pure-Erlang `erx_bytes` stand-in, tap to a jail, 512 MiB in 64 KiB sends, median of 3; `~/erxos-history/net-bench-freebsd-2026-11-09/`) erx_net sent at 767 Mbit/s and received at 1,115, against stock `gen_tcp` at 7,098 and 7,159 and iperf3 at 6,420; the connection process saturated one scheduler on send. The profile (`~/erxos-history/net-perf-freebsd-2026-11-09/`) spreads the cost over the per-segment step, and [design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-fast-path) (runs, the merge, the lean step) is the fix. Settled by that design's bulk benchmark meeting its target. A FreeBSD-stack baseline through the same tap needs an address on the host's side of bridge0, a test-host setting change that waits for approval.
* **[Medium] network, one interface process per interface as a ceiling** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#residuals)): every frame of an interface is decoded in one process. Settled by the minimum-size frame rate one interface process keeps up with, on erXos's kernel and the Pi 4; fix: one interface process per receive queue.
* **[Medium] network, checksums in the copy** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#checksums-in-the-copy)): the design replaces the separate checksum passes (the TCP encoder's in the connection, `decode/2`'s in the interface) with a sum computed in the copy. With the C primitive, `erx_bytes` held 3.1 percent of the connection's send samples ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#where-a-segments-cost-goes)); a probe puts a 1,480-byte sum at 0.30 us on the development host, about a sixth of a scheduler per direction at 550,000 frames a second. Measure before and after: the stage breakdown of plan.md's GN-QEMU bulk transfer under HVF (completion, classification, checksum, step, encoding, posting; record which finisher the device took); the FreeBSD send and receive over tap and netmap with `erx_bytes:inet_csum/2`'s and the NIF copies' shares; and `erx_inet_copy_sum` against a copy then `erx_inet_sum` in one C call over 64 to 9,014 bytes on the development and FreeBSD test hosts. If the fused loop is slower, copy then sum in the same call.
* **[Medium] network, fixed buffer sizes and connection credit** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#credited-queues)): the send buffer grows by FreeBSD's autosizing (`tcp_sndbuf_autoscale`) within the grant's `buffers`, measured (bulk-send 2,644 to 5,100 Mbit/s on the FreeBSD x86-64 test host; erxos-history `net-autosnd-2026-11-10`); the receive buffer stays at 64 KiB, with receive autosizing left out since larger receive windows overflow the test path's tap queue, and credit stays at 256 items. Settled for the receive side by bulk-recv with the buffer at 64 KiB to 2 MiB over a medium whose queue holds the window, and credit at 64 to 1,024.
* **[Medium] network, send credit's messages and a round trip per datagram sent** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): a stream's pieces cross the lane with no reply within credit granted by the half-buffer rule, one credit message per half buffer acknowledged and one more each time a stalled peer leaves less than half free; a datagram send is one lane round trip. Settled by bulk throughput with 1 KiB, 64 KiB and 16 MiB sends against line rate, counting credit messages per MiB, outlet refusals and the socket process's wait, and datagrams per second on one socket. Fixes: a larger grant step for streams; send credit for datagrams with outcomes reported at the next call, which changes `gen_udp:send`'s result and needs a design.
* **[Medium] network, the refused sender's notification against the bell** ([design-runtime.md](design-runtime.md#connections-in-erts)): a socket process refused a send waits for `{erx_lane_ready, Node}`, which edit 67 sends where ERTS resumes a suspended sender; without the edit, a bell process per destination makes a suspending no-op send, a lane frame per round and a local message each way per refusal. Settled by a 64 MiB bulk send and rr-64 with the owner in a test domain on QEMU under HVF and a lane small enough to pass the busy limit: throughput, refusals per MiB and time from the queue falling under the limit to the next accepted send. The edit is made only if it beats the bell beyond the rounds' spread.
* **[Low] network, a connection's first send waits for the first contact** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): the first send credit follows the socket process's first message, so a `send` right after `connect` or `accept` waits one lane round trip. Settled by `connect` to the first byte at the peer on QEMU under HVF, against an open's answer carrying the first send credit, the change to make if a connection-per-request workload shows the wait.
* **[Medium] network, the copy of a send's pieces** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): the socket process makes every piece a binary of its own size, so a queued piece keeps only its bytes alive, and a send of one large binary makes one more pass over all its bytes beside the lane's copy. Settled by bulk throughput and CPU per byte with 64 KiB, 1 MiB and 64 MiB sends of one binary and of iodata of 4 KiB binaries, against the copy disabled, on the FreeBSD x86-64 test host and QEMU under HVF.
* **[Medium] network, the socket process's hop** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): each delivery is a local message to the owner, each call one to the socket process, each half buffer received a credit message across the lane, and each socket holds a `gen_statem` in the owner's domain; a design review's estimate of a few percent of a core is not a measurement. Settled by active-mode bulk receive (`{active, true}`, `{active, 100}`), a 1 KiB request-response loop and a 10,000-socket idle set, owner in another domain, against a build whose connection sends to a same-domain owner directly: throughput, latency, reductions per delivered byte in the owner's domain, memory per socket. Fixes: coalesced pushes when the owner is behind, less frequent credit.
* **[Medium] network, the local round trip per call through the socket process** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): a request-reply costs four local messages in the owner's domain and one lane crossing each way. A build with a reply per piece cost 8 percent on rr-64 (13,824 against 15,065 transactions a second, FreeBSD x86-64 test host, stock OTP 29; `~/erxos-history` `net-bench/1be-p9a`); a send-credit hack ([the bench](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process-exploration-and-two-domains); `net-sendcredit-hack-2026-11-10/`) cut rr-64's CPU per transaction 4.0 percent at `+S 1:1` and 7.9 percent at default flags. Settled by the bench's QEMU part when the package is built: rr-64 and a 64 MiB send with the owner in a test domain under HVF against master, counting lane crossings per transaction and outlet refusals. The hop itself is removed only when measured: within one domain on a standalone `erx_net` release, and as native code only if cross-domain measurement shows the owner's side among the larger costs (the design's decisions 16 and 17).
* **[Low] network, a send of iodata is flattened** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#copies)): `erx_gen_tcp:send/2` makes the owner's iodata one binary, copying every byte of a multi-binary send, where stock hands its port an I/O vector. Settled by bulk sends of 64 KiB iolists of 1 KiB binaries against one binary, throughput and bytes copied; fix: the socket process cuts pieces from the iodata's binaries.
* **[Low] network, packet-mode copies in the socket process** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): `erx_gen_tcp_core` joins held pieces into one binary for a packet that spans them, and a delivered packet is a sub-binary keeping its whole piece or join alive (up to 64 KiB, or the credit rule's target, about 64 MiB in a length mode with a large `packet_size`); a length-mode send copies its data behind the header (`erx_net_packet:encode/2`). Settled by `{packet, 4}` bulk of 1 MiB packets and `{packet, line}` of 60 KiB lines against `raw`, throughput and bytes copied.
* **[Low] network, the receive join on erXos** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#copies)): merged payloads stay as frame sub-binaries, so the socket process joins a multi-piece delivery: four copies per received byte against three if the device NIF's copy laid payloads end to end. Settled by CPU per byte of a bulk receive on QEMU `virt` under HVF with the join's profile share; fix: the merge in the device NIF's copy, which needs a parser of device-written bytes there.
* **[Medium] network, stretch acknowledgements from merged segments** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#a-merged-segment-in-tcp)): one ACK per merged segment of up to 45 segments, where RFC 5681 asks for one every second; with a 64 KiB receive buffer a unit can be most of the window. Settled by the bulk receive on the FreeBSD test host, merge on and off: throughput, the sender's congestion window (`tcp_log` or `siftr`), retransmissions; fix: end a unit at a segment count derived from the receive window.
* **[Medium] network, a fixed-length `recv` of a few segments** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-interface-towards-erlang-programs)): `erx_tcp_conn`'s `take/2` joins the whole receive queue for each recv that takes part of it. In the FreeBSD run (64 MiB, one run each) `recv(S, 1460)` read at 29 Mbit/s, `recv(S, 16384)` at 984, `recv(S, 0)` at 1,115. Settled by a profile of the `recv(S, 1460)` run; the socket process removes the call, and the fix still open is splitting held bytes at the length without joining the rest.
* **[Low] network, a `rcvd` step per push** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-socket-process)): every push to the socket process is followed by a `{user, {rcvd, N}}` step and an output pass. Settled by `recv(S, 16384)` and `recv(S, 65536)` receives, or a 64 MiB host receive through the test pipe, with a step per push and one per quarter buffer; fix: the latter, as FreeBSD's `tcp_output` updates the window only on a significant change.
* **[Medium] network, SACK walks over unmerged SACKed ranges.** `erx_tcp_sendlog` merges SACKed neighbours only when their segments line up (`merge_down/2`), so app-limited writes, an ACK inside a segment or a path MTU drop leave several ranges, which every repeating ACK walks in `sack_ranges/4` and `mark_lost/3`. Settled by recovery over a 1,000-segment window at 10 percent loss with app-limited writes, reductions per ACK against one range per stretch; fix: a SACKed range keeps a list of segment sizes.
* **The send log's walks during SACK recovery.** `erx_tcp_sendlog`'s `next_seg/2` walks from `snd_una` past SACKed and retransmitted ranges, `mark_lost/3` walks down from the highest SACKed range, and `timeout/2` and `resend_holes/1` rewrite every range, each linear in ranges per call. Settled by a host benchmark of one recovery over a 10,000-segment window; fix: a hint of the first range not yet retransmitted, as FreeBSD's `sackhint.nexthole`.
* **The send buffer's walk for each segment.** `erx_tcp_sndbuf:range/4` lists the owner's binaries and skips to the offset on every call, once per segment from `erx_tcp_output`, so C segments over N binaries cost C * N. Settled by one output pass at credit 64 over a full buffer written in 1-byte and 1,460-byte sends; fixes: chunks in a tree keyed by offset, or an iterator one pass advances.
* **A segment of more than 64 binaries walks the send buffer twice** (`erlang/apps/erx_net/src/erx_tcp_output.erl`, `run/6`): output asks `range/4` for at most 64 binaries, and when they cannot hold one segment asks again unbounded. Settled by 1-byte `gen_tcp:send/2` calls queued before the first ACK against 1 KiB binaries; fix: coalesce the buffer's binaries at append.
* **[Low] network, runs cut short by many small sends** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#output-makes-runs)): a run holds at most 64 send-buffer binaries, so 100-byte writes give runs of a few frames. Settled by the FreeBSD bulk send in 100-byte against 64 KiB `gen_tcp:send/2` calls, frames per run and throughput; fix: join small binaries as the buffer takes them, as FreeBSD's `sbcompress`.
* **[Medium] network, the driver-to-interface hop for request-reply** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#processes-and-batching)): a received segment passes driver and interface before its connection, about a microsecond of a small transaction. Settled by the request-reply profile on the FreeBSD test host once the lean step lands; fixes: the driver classifies over the interface's published view, or the transport runs inside the interface.
* **[Low] network, scheduler placement of the driver, interface and connection** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#processes-and-batching)): with 128 schedulers a flow's three processes run on different cores and wake each other about 15 times a segment. Settled by the FreeBSD bulk and request-reply runs at default flags, at `+S 4:4`, and bound to one run queue through ERTS's undocumented `scheduler` option of `spawn_opt` and `process_flag/2`: CPU per GiB, context switches, throughput; fix: that binding.
* **[Low] network, the tap transport's system call and sum per frame** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-tap-transport)): one frame per `read` or `write`, and the transport reads each payload again to sum it. Settled by CPU per GiB of the FreeBSD bulk runs split by function; fixes: the tap's virtio-net header (`TAPSVNETHDR`) offloads, if `bridge0` and `epair` honour them, and the netmap transport.
* **[Low] network, the netmap transport's receive copy and binary per frame** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-netmap-transport)): `erx_netmap_nif:recv/1` copies each frame into a new binary. Settled by the profile of that section's transport-alone run, 60- and 1,514-byte `pkt-gen` frames on `vale0`; fix, rejected unless the share is large: lend slots to binaries with spare buffers swapped in.
* **[Low] network, a NIF call per transmitted frame that is not a run** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-netmap-transport)): `erx_net_driver` hands one frame or run per `transmit/2`, so `erx_netmap` enters the NIF per plain frame and syncs once per mailbox pass. Settled by the driver's 60-byte transmit rate to `pkt-gen` against `pkt-gen` to `pkt-gen`; fix: a callback taking the backlog in one call.
* **[Low] network, frames copied only to be dropped for want of credit** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-netmap-transport)): past the interface's receive credit, netmap still copies each batch before the driver drops it as `no_room`. Settled by CPU per dropped frame under a 60-byte `pkt-gen` flood with credit full; fix: skip the receive call while credit is full.
* **[Low] network, a poll-thread wake per empty-to-busy ring** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#the-netmap-transport)): at low rates each frame finding the ring empty costs a poll-thread wake and a message to the driver. Settled by `pkt-gen` ping-pong latency over `vale0` against `pkt-gen` to itself; fix: a short bounded re-check before arming.
* **[Low] network, a reassembled datagram copied and decoded again** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#ipv4-and-arp)): `erx_ip4_reass` and `erx_ip6_reass` return a completed datagram as one copy of up to 64 KiB, which the codec decodes again. Settled by CPU per byte of a fragmented UDP receive against handing over the fragments without the copy.
* **[Low] network, eviction from a full neighbour table** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#ipv4-and-arp)): `erx_lltable`'s `room/1` walks all 1,024 entries, two callbacks each, for every new neighbour once the table is full, refused ones included, at an on-link sender's line rate. Settled by time per learnt ARP request into a full against an empty table; fix: an eviction index of rank, age and key beside the deadline index.
* **[Low] network, ETS reads on every output pass** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#framing)): a connection reads the configuration generation and its next hop's neighbour row from ETS on every output. Settled by time per frame of a bulk send with and without caching them across a step's frames.
* `erx_net_frame:snapshot/3` rebuilds the route table with `erx_net_route:put/2` and sorts the addresses on every configuration generation change, for every reader, and an unconnected UDP socket builds a path per datagram. Settled by a path rebuild per datagram over a host-sized configuration (a few interfaces, tens of rows).
* **ECN alternates the frame layer's one-entry template cache** (`erlang/apps/erx_net/src/erx_net_frame.erl`, `cached_template/3`): with ECN, pure ACKs (codepoint 0) and data (ECT) interleave and rebuild the template on each change. Settled by template builds a second in an ECN-negotiated bulk send with ACKs both ways; fix: one template per codepoint.
* **[Low] network, a hibernated TIME-WAIT process** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#time-wait)): each TIME-WAIT connection is a process for 60 s. Settled by the memory of 4,096 against FreeBSD's control blocks in the same state.
* **[Low] network, credits and per-step reduction accounting** ([design-network-stack.md](design-network-stack.md#credited-queues)): each hand-over costs an `atomics:add_get/3`, each socket step two `process_info/2` reads and an `atomics:add/3`. Settled by time per frame of a bulk transfer with and without them.
* **[Low] network, an explicit bind's scan of the demultiplexing table.** `erx_net_tables` runs every explicit bind (`erx_net_bind:bind/2`) as an `ets:select/2` over every entry, connections included, even for a named port; port 0 then checks up to 55,536 ephemeral ports with `avail/2`, all in the owner of the shared tables, delaying other binds and ended grants' cleanup. Settled on the host by a bind with 10,000 connections in the table, and a port-0 bind refused for a grant with no ephemeral port left.

## Per-byte primitives and crypto

* **[Low] per-byte primitives, `erx_bytes:crc32c/2` runs the portable code only.** Four bits per table step, not the CPU's CRC-32C instructions that [the design](design-runtime.md#per-byte-primitives) puts behind the architecture layer. Measure a `tools/erx bench fs` read of an EROFS image on HVF against the call stubbed to a constant. Fixes: a 256-entry byte table, slicing by eight, then the AArch64 instructions by feature detection, the portable code as their oracle.
* **[Low] per-byte primitives, `erx_bytes:sha256/1` runs FreeBSD's portable C only,** not FEAT_SHA256 ([the design](design-runtime.md#per-byte-primitives)); a probe gave about 240 us over 64 KiB on the development host. Measure the code server's blob checks during a boot that loads every module by hash, on HVF, against the check stubbed; if visible, add the instructions by feature detection with the portable code as oracle. Time one 64 KiB call on each board too, since `erx_bytes_bif.c` charges it a quarter slice from the host's number.
* **[Low] per-byte primitives, `erx_bytes:ed25519_verify/3`'s cost on slower targets.** A probe gave about 85 us for the curve and 105 more for SHA-512 over 64 KiB on the development host, from which `erx_bytes_bif.c` charges 360 to 440 reductions; on the Pi 4 or under TCG one call may hold a scheduler for several slices, and each installed package adds one at boot. Measure one call over 0 bytes and 64 KiB on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4, and the boot package scan with 16 packages. Fixes: recharge from the target's number, or split the SHA-512 into trapped pieces as `sha256_update/2` does.
* **[Low] per-byte primitives, `erx_bytes:inet_csum/2` and `siphash24/2` run portable C only,** not the instructions [the network design](design-network-stack.md#where-c-is-used-and-what-is-measured) puts behind the architecture layer. Probes on the development host: a 64 KiB checksum takes about 2.5 us, SipHash-2-4 110 us, a 36-byte SipHash (a SYN cookie) 60 ns; `erx_bytes_reductions` (`erx_net`'s `c_src/erx_iodata.h`) charges from these, plus a reduction per 64 list cells, a guess eight times below ERTS's `iolist_size/1` rate. Outside erXos the portable NIF charges at least 1 percent of a slice through `enif_consume_timeslice`. Measure time per frame of the network design's bulk load on HVF against the checksum stubbed, one 64 KiB call of each and a call over 131,072 cells on each board, and the host library's cost on the FreeBSD run. Fix if visible: the AArch64 instructions by feature detection, portable code as oracle.
* [Low] per-byte primitives, the portable `erx_bytes` walks a list argument twice (`erlang/apps/erx_net/portable/erx_bytes.erl`, `iodata_fits/1`): on stock OTP the Erlang check walks a list before the NIF walks it again; erXos's BIFs walk once. Settled by the largest accepted call with the most cells (a 64 KiB list of one-byte binaries, or nested lists near the cell limit) with and without `iodata_fits/1` on the development host; fix: bound only what the copy needs.
* **[Medium] crypto, the cost of `crypto` in the interpreter image.** The interpreter image links `crypto`'s and `asn1rt_nif`'s NIFs and OpenSSL's `libcrypto`, which command domains, and in deterministic mode every domain but the system domain, map; on the Pi 4, if it lacks the cryptographic extension (unverified), AES runs in OpenSSL's NEON code ([design-crypto.md](design-crypto.md#risks-and-open-questions)). Settled by the interpreter image's size beside the system image's, and `ssh -T host cat` over 64 MiB with each offered cipher from interpreter and BeamAsm domains, on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4. ChaCha20-Poly1305, first in SSH's list, already serves clients that offer it.
* **[Medium] session release, the crypto stack's size.** The session script ships `asn1`, `crypto`, `public_key` and `ssh`, about 4.4 MiB of beams (public_key 2924 KiB, ssh 1400, crypto 176, asn1 16), in every image with a session template, the site image included. Measure the site image's memory with and without them; if it matters, ship them only in development manifests' session scripts.
* **[Low] sessions and apps, `ssl` and `inets` on every code path.** The `session` and `app` boot scripts load `asn1`, `crypto`, `public_key`, `ssl` and `inets` in every session and app ([design-crypto.md](design-crypto.md#releases)), loaded at start in embedded mode. Settled by `tools/erx boot`'s time to root's session prompt and a session's memory in `/proc` under HVF at one CPU, against a script without `ssl` and `inets`, and by the kiosk's first HTTPS request on the Pi 4. Fixes: the shared code image of [design-shared-code.md](design-shared-code.md); a script without them for apps that do not need `net`.

## Display, windows and widgets

* **[Medium] display, the firmware framebuffer's second copy.** `erx_firmware_fb` (`erlang/apps/erx_fb/src/erx_firmware_fb.erl`) composes into its own screen image and `erx_memdraw:store/4` (`display/memdraw/erx_fb_store.c`) copies each changed rectangle into the framebuffer one aligned 32-bit store at a time ([design-display.md](design-display.md#the-display-device)). Settled on the Pi 4 at 1920x1080, 32-bit color: one full-screen flush from `erx_display:flush_request/3` to its answer, and the store's share against composition alone. Fix: a write-combining `mem_attr` value in `abi/erx_abi.txt`, mapped on each backend, so `erx_screen` composes straight into the framebuffer as it does into virtio-gpu's region.
* **[Medium] display, the framebuffer mapped as device memory** (`liberx/backend/fiasco/runtime_entry.c`, `framebuffer_give`): the root makes the framebuffer's object with `ERX_MEM_ATTR_DEVICE`, since the kernel interface has no write-combining attribute, so every store to the Pi's framebuffer is uncached and ungathered. Settled by MB/s of a full-screen copy into it on the Pi 4 against a normal non-cacheable mapping of the same pages.

Settled by frame times in a Doom boot on QEMU virt under HVF, with the share in the program's writes, `erx_draw_conn` and the present, against scale 1, and by a packaged program drawing a 1280 by 800 frame in a loop. Fixes: larger writes (the 64 KiB cap is the file protocol's), a smaller unscaled frame, or giving the program its window's buffer, written through a mapping, and its window's `ctl`, a change to [design-display.md](design-display.md).
* **[Medium] display, the copy path's rate** ([design-display.md](design-display.md#performance)): `erx_screen` copies changed rectangles from a client's mapped pixel area into the screen image. * **[Medium] display, a layout change in `erx_screen`.** `erx_screen_core:layout/2` (`erlang/apps/erx_display/src/erx_screen_core.erl`) tiles and compares in O(L^2 log L) for L window and decoration rectangles, and every flush waits behind it. * **[Low] display, `erx_font`'s advances on every line.** `erx_font:width/2` and `runs/2` (`erlang/apps/erx_win/src/erx_font.erl`) turn the subfont's glyph list (about 700 for a text role) into a tuple of advances on every call. Settled by laying out a window of `body` text through the Erlang renderer, `width/2` and `runs/2` per line against a tuple made once per subfont; fix: a subfont holding its glyphs as a tuple.
* **[Medium] display, the Erlang renderer's frame on a 240x240 panel** (diff, layout and draw) against LVGL's. This is LVGL's retirement gate.
* **[Low] display, a kiosk's frame under LVGL** ([design-display.md](design-display.md#kiosks-and-glasses)): LVGL's render into the mapped buffer, the `damage` report's pipe round trip, the present and the `presented` record back. Settled by a full-screen redraw on a 240 by 240 panel and at 800 by 480 and 1920 by 1080 under HVF; fix: a second buffer, so the program renders while the display shows.
* **The dashboard's ground is decoded on every redraw.** `examples/dashboard`'s ground is a 1920x1080 indexed PNG under the whole window, and erx-lvgl keeps LVGL's image caches off (`display/lvgl/lv_conf.h`), so every invalidated area (the dial each minute, the quotes on each board) inflates the whole picture to about 8.3 MB of ARGB; drawing the ground takes `erx_dashboard:init/1` about 115 ms on the host under the JIT. Measure a redraw's time and peak memory on the Pi. Fixes: a one-entry image cache in erx-lvgl, or a ground that is not a full-screen picture.
* **[Medium] display, the widget tree's difference.** `erx_ui_model:delta/2` and `apply_ops/2` (`erlang/apps/erx_ui/src/erx_ui_model.erl`) are quadratic in one node's children, and the browser renderer recomputes `delta(Sent, Pending)` for every update of a long change. Measure `delta/2` for a 1,000-node tree, flat and nested, once `TODO.md`'s children limit is set; fixes: a patience-sorting longest run, an indexed children sequence.
* **[Low] widgets, the box model's leftover sharing is quadratic in a line's children.** `erx_ui_layout:share/2` (`erlang/apps/erx_ui/src/erx_ui_layout.erl`) hands out the remaining units one pass over the line each, O(n^2) per layout; the worst valid tree has 9 lines of 1,024 weighted children with 1,023 units left over. Settled by a relayout bench at those limits on the target; fix: averages in a priority queue (O(n + r log n)).
* **[Low] widgets, the line escape in `erx_ui_wire:encode/2`.** Every widget text passes `erx_text:escape_line/2` in Erlang, a piece at a time, on each encode of a changed text (up to 4,096 bytes for a label, 256 items for a list). Settled by `erx_ui_server:tree/2` applying a full list and label against the same with `encode_raw/2`, on the host and erXos; fix: a fast path in `erx_text` copying a run of printable ASCII whole.
* **[Low] display, decoding a widget's PNG image in the Erlang renderer** ([design-display.md](design-display.md#widget-trees)): an `image` node's 48 KiB PNG is inflated and unfiltered before `y` loads it; `erx_inflate` decodes each Huffman code a bit at a time, as puff does, and sorts lengths per dynamic block, so a hostile stream of tiny dynamic blocks costs far more than in zlib, and `erx_png` works a byte and a pixel at a time. Settled by a 48 KiB decode on HVF and the Pi 4. Fixes: a table-driven multi-bit decode in `erx_inflate`; unfiltering in C beside `erx_memdraw`.
* **[Low] desktop, a graph's stroke mask** (`erx_mask:stroke/4`, [design-desktop-programs.md](design-desktop-programs.md)): a probe gave 22 to 24 ms for 240 random samples in 300x60 at width 1.5 and 3.0 ms for a 60-sample sparkline in 100x20 on the host's stock OTP JIT; a pixel's time grows with the square of the segments reaching it. Settled by the same graphs in the stats renderer on HVF and the Pi 4; fix: thin a graph to one sample per pixel column.
* **[Medium] find and act, the palette's time to appear.** A palette opened by key or gesture ([design-find-act.md](design-find-act.md#performance)) makes a window, allocates its buffer from the session's pixel area, lays out and draws with the Erlang renderer and waits for the first present, all after the key. Settled by the bound key's down record to the first present's answer, under HVF and TCG at one CPU and on a Pi 4 by hand. Fixes: one palette window per display kept hidden between uses; a first frame drawn from the entries before member listings answer.

## Web client

* **[Low] web, every browser message crosses the system domain.** The host tool's front end reaches the web domain through the host link's `web` facet (`erx_hostlink_web`): each message a call from a host link worker to the facet and on to the web domain's relay, each answer a cast back, two cross-domain copies per message and one call at a time per host. Settled by a browser's `next` to its `update` for a 64 KiB record over the host link on HVF, against the update's time inside the web domain; fix: the web domain answers the host link's process directly.
* **[Low] web, gate GW on a board's UART** ([design-web.md](design-web.md#risks-and-open-questions)): GW's UART numbers come from a host-side pacer at 11,520 bytes a second each way, standing in for 115,200 baud. Settled by `tools/erx bench web` over a board's own UART host link at M12, against the pacer's 1,095 ms for the 1,000-child listing; fix if much slower: a record difference message.
* **[Low] web, `erx_ui_model:pair/3`, `delta/2` and `erx_ui_schema` for a 1,000-node tree,** and a tree's first attachment over several 64 KiB updates. Settled by time per call on HVF and the attachment's time over the UART host link; fix: keep the delta from the server's operations instead of comparing models.
* **[Low] web, the client's first load and the bundle's size,** over the host tool and the network front end. Settled by bytes served and time to the first rendered view; fix: split the bundle by route.

## Shell, console and logging

* **[Medium] console, input read into the tty a unit at a time.** The system console reads its line into a tty of its own (`erx_console`, `erx_tty`), which takes, echoes and stores input a unit at a time: a 17 KB line typed at the Erlang shell took 11.0 s to reach `system_domain`'s echo-facet checkpoint, against 0.75 s at the commit before the console read through the tty (probe: Apple M3 Max, macOS 27.0.1, HVF, 1 CPU, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, the development build `tools/erx check-qemu` makes at its default flags; measured at `b414b9f9d` with the tty's input bound raised to 64 KiB so the line fit, against `e1536b116`, the parent of `92bb11afd`, which made the console read through the tty). A person types far slower, but a paste, a serial upload or a harness's line pays about 0.6 ms a byte. Measure the time from the first byte written to the console's UART to the shell's read of a 4,000-byte line, and to the last echoed byte, on QEMU `virt` (HVF, 1 CPU), at `e1536b116` and at the head, with the tty's own cost (`erx_tty:input/3` over the same bytes on the host) beside it. Fixes: take every byte the UART has ready in one `erx_tty:input/3` call and send its echo as one write, or batch the console's reads behind the line discipline's pure core.
* **[Low] shell, a process per parsed unit.** The evaluator parses each unit through `erx_shell_parse:next_bounded/3`, which spawns a child with a fixed 8 MiB heap and copies the tree back as its exit reason ([design-shell.md](design-shell.md#the-language)); the receive of its `'DOWN'` has no receive marker, so it scans the whole mailbox. Measure a script of 10,000 one-command lines and one of 4 KiB units, through `next_bounded/3` and `next/2`, with pending stage messages in the mailbox. Fixes: parse a run of short units in one child, or parse in the evaluator when the window's worst heap fits its spare heap.
* **[Medium] shell, the evaluator's heap per word of a list.** Under the 4 MiB evaluator heap, `k=(); while(! ~ $#k 5000) k=($k x)` is killed though a thousand iterations fit (`erx_shell_eval_tests`): each `k=($k x)` rebuilds the list through per-word chunk lists, and dead copies in the old heap count until a major collection. Measure peak `total_heap_size` against list length on the gates' stock OTP 29.1.2 (`build/oracle/jit/install`). Fixes: a lower `fullsweep_after` for evaluators; skipping chunk lists for words from values.
* **[Medium] shell, `~` and `switch` on a long subject.** The matcher turns the subject into a character list per pattern, so `~ $x *b*` is killed on a 100 KB subject while 50 KB passes. Measure peak heap against subject length; fix: match over the binary with byte offsets and a pattern compiled once.
* [Low] `erlang/apps/erx_shell/src/erx_shell.erl`, `end_line/3`: every unit the command shell runs, a refused one included, ends with a pass over `erlang:processes()` with a `process_info/2` each. Settled by an empty line's time at the console with the domain's usual process count and ten times as many.
* **[Low] shell, ping's request data.** `erx_cmd_ping:payload/2` builds the fill with `lists:seq` per request, a 65,507-element list a millisecond at `-s 65507 -i 0.001`, where FreeBSD fills once. Settled by reductions and send rate of `ping -s 65507 -i 0.001 -c 1000` to slirp's gateway under HVF, against the fill built once at start.
* **[Low] shell, the console's one-byte reads.** The console reads one byte per ask while the group's line editor waits for a key ([design-shell.md](design-shell.md), the terminal relay), about six messages per typed byte. Settled by pasting 4 KiB at the prompt under HVF at one CPU against a program reading its tty raw; fix: a read answering every buffered byte while the editor is in plain insertion.
* **A stopped session's resends** (`erlang/apps/erx/src/erx_console.erl`, `erlang/apps/erx_session/src/erx_tty_link.erl`): a session not connected to the console resends its empty held output once a second for life, across its console facet into `erx_console`, which ignores it. Settled by the system domain's reductions and the UART's latency with 1, 16 and 64 stopped sessions; fix: a master that tells the tty it holds the output.
* **[Low] vi, a one-line scroll rewrites the screen.** `vi` compares its new screen with the old row by row ([design-vi.md](design-vi.md#drawing)), so `j` at the window's bottom rewrites about 2 KB at 80 by 24, an estimated 170 ms at 115,200 bit/s. Settled by timing held `j` at the bottom of a long file over the UART and the host link's `console` line; fix: detect the shift and scroll text rows with the terminal's region and `CSI S`/`CSI T` before writing the rows that still differ.
* **[Medium] runtime, the log sink under a flood of events.** `erx_log_h:log/2` sends every record without `logger_olp`'s queue checks, and `erx_syslog_core`'s rate drops past 16 KiB a second only once the record is queued in `erx_syslog`. Settled by a crash loop logging large reports in the system domain and in a domain: `erx_syslog`'s queue length, a `write`'s answer latency, and the proxy's queue and kernel calls per second, with and without a queue-length bound in `log/2`.
* **[Low] log service, the kernel ring's 100 ms poll** (`erx_syslog`, [design-log.md](design-log.md#performance)): an idle machine wakes the service ten times a second. Settled by wakeups per second against an IRQ-object signal on erXos's kernel.
* **[Low] log service, a flood at 256 domains' full rate** (`erx_syslog`, [design-log.md](design-log.md#performance)): 4 MiB a second rendered and escaped by one process. Settled by lines per second and a `write`'s latency under it.
* **[Low] log service, a record past the rate is escaped before it is dropped** (`erx_syslog_core`, `write/4`): a domain at its rate still makes the system domain escape every record, up to 16 of 4 KiB per `write`, and its name with each; since escaping never shortens text, a record whose head and raw text already pass the rate could be dropped unescaped. Settled by `write/4`'s time for a domain past its rate under the flood above.
* **[Low] log store, a sync per batch on littlefs over SD** (`erx_syslog_writer`, [design-log.md](design-log.md#the-writer)): up to one append and sync a second per file, two files. Settled by the store's write time and the card's write traffic on the Pi 4, against 10 s batches.
* **[Low] log store, a process per batch** (`erx_syslog_writer`, [design-log.md](design-log.md#the-writer)): each batch opens, reads the last byte and size of, appends to, syncs and closes each file, about six requests instead of two, at most once a second per family. Settled by write time per batch on the Pi 4 against a writer that keeps its files open.

## Apps, packages and SSH

* **[Low] apps, the quantities of the rows the build derives are not measured.** An app template's facet rows take their `quantity` lines from their services (`abi/services/windows.txt`, `net.txt`, `files.txt`; docs/design-abi.md, "Services, facets and their parameter schemas"): windows and files `max_request` 131072, `in_flight` 16, a 262144-word grant heap; net 64, 16 and 65536 words; all 100000 reductions a second, copied from the development manifest's hand-written rows. Too small a heap ends an app's grant under load; too large lets `max` apps each hold that much of the server's memory. Settled by running the kiosk dashboard and Doom as packages, recording each grant process's peak heap and its server's memory; the values become the peaks with headroom.
* **[Medium] apps, code loaded from a package.** An app's modules load from its package's EROFS image at `/app` through `erx_verity`, 4 KiB blocks read through the file protocol and hashed, crossing to the storage domain for an installed package ([design-packaging.md](design-packaging.md#performance)), where a manifest app loads from the release slice. Settled by `tools/erx bench app-start` with `examples/dashboard` as a package and as a release app, under HVF. Fixes: larger reads per call; the shared prepared code of [design-shared-code.md](design-shared-code.md).
* **[Low] apps, a copy into `/apps` and a read at a mount of a package go through the system domain.** A package's bytes cross from the administrator's session to the system domain's `apps` tree and on to the storage domain, and a read at a mount of `/apps/NAME.app` comes back the same way, each block through a fid `erx_pkg_io` opens for it ([design-packaging.md](design-packaging.md#apps)). Settled by `cp` of a 32 MiB package into `/apps` and `cat` of a 28 MiB file at its mount, under HVF. Fixes: larger writes and reads per message; a fid held per open package.
* **[Low] apps, walks and reads of `/apps` behind a package checkpoint.** `erx_pkg_io` takes one request at a time and finishes a begun checkpoint first, so every walk, `getattr` and read of `/apps` waits behind a slow checkpoint during install or removal ([design-packaging.md](design-packaging.md)). Settled by readers' worst latency while a package installs, on Fiasco and on F2FS; fix: answer path-table lookups apart from storage requests.
* **[Medium] SSH, a login's latency.** Every SSH login starts a session domain from the principal's template, and a key login adds a vault question for the principal's keys and an Ed25519 check in the system domain's verifier ([design-ssh.md](design-ssh.md#risks-and-open-questions)). Settled by `tools/erx bench ssh-login`, to be written: TCP connect to the first byte of `echo hi`'s output, key and passphrase logins, on QEMU `virt` under HVF and the Pi 4, beside a console login's session start. If the session start dominates, the fix belongs to domain creation.

## Boot and images

* **[Medium] boot, the development image's display domain.** `config/manifest/development.txt` configures a display domain, a fifth interpreter runtime started on every boot with or without a gpu, loading its own `kernel` and `stdlib` and making and zeroing the gpu's 9 MiB of DMA memory. With no gpu it moves `release-shell` from 362.1 to 386.1 ms median (11 HVF boots each; Apple M3 Max, QEMU 11.1.3, Homebrew clang 23.1.3, one CPU, the interactive image; ~/erxos-history/boot-devdisplay-2026-11-13). Settle where it costs with `tools/erx boot`'s time to root's prompt and `tools/erx bench boot` under HVF and TCG at one CPU, against a manifest without the row. Fixes if it shows on the gate's cells: the shared code image of [design-shared-code.md](design-shared-code.md); leaving the row out of suites' images that do not need it.
* **[Low] boot, root-identity at 512 MiB.** Raising the QEMU virt development machine from 256 to 512 MiB moves `root-identity` from 144.9 to 161.5 ms median (11 HVF boots each, `erxos-banner` unchanged; same host and tools; ~/erxos-history/boot-ram512-2026-11-13). The first suspect is memory setup in Fiasco or sigma0, which grows with RAM. Settled by timing marks splitting the span between the banner and root-identity at both sizes.
* **[Low] boot, the archive move on the Raspberry Pi.** When the stand-in root lands on the archive, bootstrap moves the archive (about 60 MiB and more) behind it (`move_archive` in `fiasco/bootstrap/server/src/erx_image.cc`, docs/design-fiasco.md "Boot handoff") before the kernel starts; on the Pi the first stage may run with MMU and data cache off (`Cache::Data::enabled()`), unverified. Settled on the Pi 4 by the serial log's time from the move line to the kernel's banner, with and without the move. Fixes if it costs seconds: move with the data cache on, or link the Pi's root above its single file.
* **[Low] Fiasco boot, hashing with the MMU off.** `bootstrap` hashes the index and the files it places with portable BLAKE3 before the MMU, so with the data cache off; the harness's 50 ms serial reads cannot see it on a 1 MiB image. Measure `bootstrap`'s first line to the kernel's banner with serial timestamps on HVF and the Pi 4, with an image the size of the first real system. Fix: turn the caches on before hashing.
* **The production image's `NOTICES` and Fiasco's audit** (`tools/lib/erx/production_image.rb`): `NOTICES` is 0.8 MiB of the 23.6 MiB development boot image, each license text once, touched page by page at boot; one pack spends about 5 minutes re-auditing Fiasco on a loaded host. Measure the root's boot time with and without those pages, and the audit's time on an idle host.
